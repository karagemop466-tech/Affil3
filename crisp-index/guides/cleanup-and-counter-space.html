Living with it · Ownership costs

How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

By The Crisp Index Editorial DeskNovember 20, 20266 min readCluster: Living with it

Nobody returns an air fryer because the fries were bad. They return it because it was a nuisance to clean, or because it ate the counter. Both costs are knowable before you buy — if you go looking for them.

Cost one: the cleanup you’ll actually do

Separate the daily job from the weekly one:

  • Daily: the basket, tray, or rack that touches food. If that part isn’t easy — awkward shape, coating you’re afraid to scrub — the machine loses.
  • Weekly or so: heating elements, crumb trays, doors and glass, the greasy corners you only notice on Sunday.
  • Seasonally: deep-clean the fan intake area and any splatter zones behind the tray. Cheaper machines make this impossible. Ask before you buy.

Read “dishwasher-safe” claims carefully

Observed listings on our queue advertise “Dishwasher-Safe Frying Baskets” — note it’s the baskets (quoted verbatim in the register). Listing claims are manufacturer wording, not our test result. Three questions to ask:

  • Which specific parts are dishwasher-safe — and on which rack?
  • What does the manufacturer say about nonstick care and detergent? Coatings have a lifespan; the manual is the only source that matters.
  • What happens to the finish after a year of dishwasher cycles? Owner Q&A threads on the listing are the honest place to look.

Cost two: the footprint you’ll live with

The tape test

  • Tape the machine’s width and depth on the counter — many listings publish dimensions in the product information table at the bottom of the page.
  • Add the door swing (oven-style) or basket pull-out (basket-style). If the tape zone overlaps the sink, stove, or your only prep space, it’s a no.
  • Measure vertical clearance to the cabinet above — you need room to lift a basket or open a door, not just to fit the box.
  • Check the heat clearance the manual demands on each side; machines that hug the wall need less space but live hotter.
  • Open the cabinets: where do the racks, trays, and the second basket live when they’re not in the machine? Accessories without a home become clutter with a receipt.

Where the queue stands today

The queue currently holds one “Easy View Windows” dual-basket machine, one French-door “Multi-Function” oven, one “XL Nonstick Dishwasher-Safe Basket” air fryer, and a probe thermometer — all wording quoted from the observed listings with sources in the test queue. Which parts survive your dishwasher is a question we ask before we recommend keeping any of them.

Skip the purchase if…

  • …the food-contact parts are “hand wash only” and you know yourself.
  • …the machine fits on paper but not with the door open.
  • …the accessories have no storage and you don’t have a drawer to spare.
