Campaign ledger

Starting from nothing, on purpose

Empty by design. No authorised first-party report has been supplied, so the ledger has zero rows and no totals.

The provenance rule

A row may be added only from an authorised first-party report — an Associates export the owner supplies, or a report from a platform the owner controls — and each row must record where it came from. A site click is not an order. Nothing here may be estimated, modelled, scraped or back-filled.

What each column means

ColumnWhat goes in it
period_startFirst day of the reporting window, ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD).
period_endLast day of the reporting window, inclusive.
sourceThe report this row came from, named exactly as the system names it, e.g. 'Amazon Associates — Link Type report'.
channelWhere the traffic came from: organic search, direct, RSS, a named social post, an email send.
eligible_sessionsSessions the report counts as eligible for attribution, as the report defines it. Never derived by subtraction from another column.
clicksOutbound clicks the report attributes to this site and this window.
reported_ordersOrders the report attributes to this site and this window. Orders, not revenue, and only from the first-party report.
provenancePath or reference to the underlying export, its export date, and who exported it.
notesAnything that qualifies the row: a tracking gap, a currency or marketplace caveat, a known duplicate.

What will never appear in it

  • Revenue or commission estimates of any kind.
  • Conversion rates calculated from modelled traffic.
  • Traffic forecasts.
  • Numbers copied from a screenshot without the underlying export.
  • Rows assembled from two reports with different windows.

The reason for the strictness is practical rather than moral. A ledger that mixes an exported report with an estimate cannot be used to make a decision, because there is no way to tell which row caused the result. An empty ledger is a weaker asset than a populated one and a far better one than a populated fiction.
