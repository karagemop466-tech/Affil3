Cooking a holiday meal with less counter space than you need

Four weeks a year the oven has a queue. A plan for the fortnight when counter space is the scarcest ingredient.

By The Counter Desk editorial desk· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 6 minute read

The short version

  • Clear the counter before you buy anything; two appliances need two footprints.
  • A second cooking box frees the main oven, which is the real bottleneck in November.
  • The bird's temperature is the one number that cannot slip.
  • Cook the things that hold, and roast the things that do not.

The holiday problem is not a shortage of recipes. It is a shortage of hot air and flat surfaces, for about ten days, in a kitchen built for a Tuesday.

Name the bottleneck before you shop

Most small kitchens have one oven, four burners and about two feet of usable counter. On the day, the oven is the queue: the bird takes hours, the sides want 425 °F, and the rolls want the oven at the exact moment the bird is resting. Anything that moves a side dish out of the main oven buys back an hour.

A countertop air fryer oven does exactly that, at the cost of counter. Which is the trade: an hour of oven time for a permanent 16.9 by 15.8 inch rectangle, if you pick the Emeril unit S12. Measure the rectangle before you decide the hour is worth it.

Clear the counter first, then measure

  1. Take everything off the counter that is not used weekly — the stand mixer, the knife block, the fruit bowl — and find it another home for December.
  2. Measure depth, width and height of what is left, including the space under the cabinets.
  3. Check where the nearest outlet is and whether the cord reaches without an extension lead across a walkway.
  4. Decide which appliance lives out for the fortnight and which goes in the cupboard.

Give the second appliance the sides, not the bird

The single most useful division of labour: the main oven does the bird and anything that needs to baste in its fat; the countertop unit does the tray of vegetables, the stuffing balls and the rolls. Vegetables at 400 °F for twenty minutes are a better use of a second chamber than a whole turkey, which needs the volume and the even heat of the main oven.

The number that cannot slip

Poultry is 165 °F minimum internal, measured in the thigh, the wing and the thickest part of the breast, avoiding bone S07 S08. Everything else on the table can be held, reheated or served warm. That one number decides whether the meal is safe, so it is the one place to spend a thermometer rather than a timer.

A wireless probe left in the breast or thigh while the bird roasts answers the question continuously; an instant-read answers it in a second each time you open the oven. Both are covered in the thermometer guide on this site, including the difference between the ambient rating and the inserted-probe rating that catches people out at exactly this time of year.

Cook what holds, roast what does not

  • Holds well: braises, stuffing, root vegetables, gravy, cranberry sauce, anything served from a pot.
  • Does not hold: anything fried or air-fried and meant to be crisp, roast potatoes, Yorkshire puddings, anything with a crust.
  • Cook the second list last, in the appliance nearest the table, and serve it straight from the basket.

That is the whole plan: clear, measure, move the sides out of the oven, and put the one thermometer where it matters. No new appliance is required to do it — but if you were going to buy one anyway, buy it in early November and use it twice before the day, not on it.
