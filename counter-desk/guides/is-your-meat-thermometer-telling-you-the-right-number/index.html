Is your meat thermometer telling you the right number?

The USDA targets, where to put the probe, and the two numbers a thermometer page should state but rarely does.

By The Counter Desk editorial desk· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 8 minute read

The short version

  • The target is the USDA minimum, not the number the app suggests.
  • Inserted-probe and ambient ratings are different numbers and are routinely confused.
  • Consumer thermometers are expected to be accurate within 2–4 °F.
  • Check a new thermometer in ice water before you trust it with a bird.

A thermometer is only useful if three things are true: the number is accurate, the probe is in the right place, and you know what number you are aiming for. Two of those are free, and the third is published by the USDA.

The targets, from the USDA chart

These are minimum internal temperatures, measured with a food thermometer, before the food leaves the heat S07:

  • Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts: 145 °F, then rest at least 3 minutes.
  • Ground meats: 160 °F.
  • Ground poultry: 165 °F.
  • All poultry — breasts, whole birds, legs, thighs, wings, giblets and stuffing: 165 °F.
  • Fresh or smoked ham that is uncooked: 145 °F, then rest at least 3 minutes.
  • Fish and shellfish: 145 °F.
  • Eggs: 160 °F. Leftovers and casseroles: 165 °F.

Those rows are the chart, not a summary of it. If you cook something not on the list, the chart is the place to look, not a recipe blog.

Where the probe goes

For a whole bird, the USDA says to check three places: the innermost part of the thigh, the innermost part of the wing, and the thickest part of the breast, avoiding bone S08. For poultry parts, insert into the thickest area, avoiding bone. For a roast, midway into the thickest part, away from bone, fat and gristle. For a thin piece like a burger or a chop, insert through the side so the sensing area reaches the centre.

Bone conducts heat differently from meat, which is why 'avoid the bone' is in every one of those instructions. A reading taken against a bone can be several degrees off in either direction, and neither direction is safe to guess at.

The two numbers people confuse

A wireless probe is usually rated twice: once for the portion inserted into the food, and once for the ambient heat around it. On the CHEF iQ Sense, the inserted portion is rated 32–212 °F while the ambient sensor is rated up to 1,000 °F S17 S18. The 1,000 °F figure is what survives a grill lid or an oven; the 212 °F figure is what the metal in contact with the meat can take. It is not an oil or candy thermometer, and no amount of headline wattage changes that.

An instant-read has the opposite shape of rating. ThermoMaven's manual states a measured range of −58 to 572 °F for the F1 Stealth S21, which comfortably covers roasting and frying, but the same manual says not to leave it in the food while cooking, or in the oven, microwave or dishwasher. So the number is a range, not a licence to leave it in.

How accurate is accurate enough?

The USDA's own thermometer factsheet says most food thermometers give an accurate reading within 2 to 4 °F S08. A manufacturer claiming ±0.5 °F is claiming better than the consumer baseline; that is plausible for a calibrated thermocouple and irrelevant to whether your chicken is done. Both numbers are worth knowing, and neither replaces probe placement.

Check it in ice water before you trust it

  1. Fill a glass with ice, add water, and stir.
  2. Immerse the sensing part at least two inches without touching the glass.
  3. Wait at least 30 seconds and read. It should show 32 °F.
  4. If it is off, follow the manufacturer's calibration steps; if it cannot be calibrated, replace it.

That is the USDA method S08, and it takes a minute. A thermometer that fails it will fail on the bird too, and you will not find out until the bird is on the board.

Worth knowing

One more habit: check several pieces, not one. Size and position make pieces reach temperature at different times, and a single reading from the middle of the tray is a sample of one.
