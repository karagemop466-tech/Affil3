Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Every function on the box is a function you have to learn, clean and store. Nine questions that separate the useful ones from the brochure.

By The Counter Desk editorial desk· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 7 minute read

The short version

  • Name the three functions you will use weekly and ignore the rest.
  • A preset count is a menu, not a capability.
  • Ask what the manual documents — times, temperatures, care — not what the page advertises.
  • Box contents are contested in this category; get them in writing.

A multi-function appliance is a bet that one box can do the work of three. Sometimes it pays. The questions below are the ones that decide whether it will for you, and each one can be answered before you open your wallet.

1. Which three functions will you use every week?

Write them down. If the list is 'toast, roast, air fry', an oven-style unit is a candidate. If it is 'air fry, air fry, air fry', a basket is cheaper and takes less counter. The functions you use monthly do not belong in the decision.

2. Is the preset count a number or a menu?

A preset is a saved time-and-temperature pair. Twenty-four of them is a menu with twenty-four entries, not twenty-four capabilities. Chefman's 8-quart unit has four presets and manual controls S14; that is fewer labels and the same machine underneath. The Emeril page cannot even agree with itself on its count — '25 Cooking Pre-sets' in one place, '24 one-touch cooking presets' in another S12 — which is a useful reminder of how much a preset number is worth as a comparison.

3. Does the manual include times and temperatures for your food?

This is the single most useful document and the least advertised. A manual with a cook chart turns a new appliance into a usable one on day one. A manual without one means the first fortnight is guesswork. Both manufacturers here publish manuals; neither product page summarises whether a cook chart is inside. Ask, or download the PDF before you order.

4. Can you see the food and the timer at the same time?

Glass doors and interior lights are worth more than a preset called 'crispy'. The Emeril unit's french doors are glass S12; Chefman's dual-basket unit adds an interior light and two windows S10. Seeing the food is the difference between opening the door to check and trusting the timer.

5. What exactly is in the box?

In this category, box contents are genuinely contested. The Emeril product description says seven accessories are included, the listing title says six, and the manufacturer sells the grill plate separately on its own site S11 S12. Before ordering, ask the seller for the current in-box list for the exact model number. This is not pedantry: a missing grill plate is a second purchase.

6. Where will it live, and what has to move?

A 26-quart oven needs 16.9 inches of depth and 14.8 inches of height before the doors open S12. On a standard 24-inch-deep counter it fits with room to spare; on a narrow counter under deep cabinets it does not. Decide now whether it lives out or comes out of a cupboard, because that answer decides how often you will use it.

7. Who services it, and for how long?

Both manufacturers here state a one-year warranty on their product pages S12 S14. A warranty is only useful if there is a route to claim it and a service network behind it. Check whether support is a phone line, a web form, or a repair partner, and keep the order confirmation.

8. Are the parts you will wear out sold separately?

Racks, baskets and trays scratch, and nonstick coatings eventually give up. If replacements are not sold, the appliance's life is the life of its worst part. Manufacturer accessory pages are the place to check, and the Emeril site's separate grill-plate listing is an example of a part you can buy later S12.

9. What does it cost you in noise, heat and cleaning?

A 1700 W appliance in a small kitchen is a space heater with a fan. It is loud, and it warms the room. Neither figure is published by any manufacturer in this set, so treat the absence as something to discover from a returned unit or a friend's kitchen rather than something to assume away.

Worth knowing

The short version: three weekly functions, a manual with numbers in it, and a counter it actually fits on. Everything else is a label.
