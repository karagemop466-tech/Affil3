Decision board

Suggestions awaiting a choice

Every item here is a decision waiting on the owner. Nothing is implemented until an option is chosen; the site keeps working with whichever option is marked current. Reply with the suggestion ID and the letter, for example 'S-03 B'.

S-01 · Two publications in one repository

Current: A

  • A. Keep the earlier /editorial/ edition live but excluded from search, and index only the root site (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing is lost and there is one indexed site; the old edition is still public if someone has the URL.
  • B. Retire /editorial/ entirely and redirect its URLs to the equivalent root pages.
    Tradeoff: Cleanest signal for search engines; a redirect map has to be written and tested first.
  • C. Merge the better parts of the old edition into the root site and remove the duplicate pages.
    Tradeoff: Best content outcome, most work, and the old edition's data conflicts with the root data and must be reconciled.

Session recommendation: B once the redirect map is written. Two editions of a five-page site is more surface than the content justifies.

S-02 · Buy buttons while links are untested

Current: A

  • A. Keep the buy buttons, labelled as provisional and pending a tracking check (current).
    Tradeoff: The site can earn from day one, but an untested link that silently fails credits nothing.
  • B. Hide every buy button until gate G-02 passes.
    Tradeoff: Maximum compliance confidence; the site earns nothing until the owner tests.

Session recommendation: A, but test within the first week. A page that cannot be clicked is a brochure.

S-03 · Where the analytics collector runs

Current: D

  • A. Run tools/collector.mjs on a small VPS the owner controls, behind HTTPS.
    Tradeoff: Full control of raw data; the owner runs a server and patches it.
  • B. Run it as a serverless function on a platform that supports Node.
    Tradeoff: No server to patch; cold starts and platform limits on storage.
  • C. Skip self-hosting and use a privacy-focused hosted analytics service.
    Tradeoff: Fastest to start; one more third party, and the data leaves your control.
  • D. Stay inactive until there is traffic worth measuring (current).
    Tradeoff: Zero maintenance; decisions stay on instinct, which is exactly what the brief asks to avoid.

Session recommendation: B. It removes the server maintenance objection without handing the data away.

S-04 · Pricing display

Current: A

  • A. Show no prices; every button reads 'Check the current price on Amazon' (current).
    Tradeoff: Always accurate, and compliant with the price clause; readers have to click to know a budget.
  • B. Enroll in the Creators API and display Amazon-served prices with a timestamp.
    Tradeoff: Better for budget-led shoppers; more engineering, and a stale price is a compliance risk.
  • C. Show a coarse price band instead of a figure.
    Tradeoff: Useful and stable; still a claim that needs checking, and it ages badly.

Session recommendation: A until traffic justifies the Creators API work. Then B.

S-05 · Publishing cadence

Current: A

  • A. One new guide every two weeks (current).
    Tradeoff: Sustainable; slow to build a topic cluster.
  • B. One new guide per week.
    Tradeoff: Faster topical coverage; needs a real writing slot weekly.
  • C. A batch of eight guides before launch, then monthly.
    Tradeoff: Reaches a useful site size quickly; risks thin pages if the batch is rushed.

Session recommendation: A, at a quality bar high enough that every guide earns its place. Ten thin guides are worse than four good ones.

S-06 · Reader capture

Current: A

  • A. No email capture yet; the RSS feed is the only subscription route (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing to maintain and no consent obligations; no direct channel to readers.
  • B. Add a single-field email capture with double opt-in on guide pages.
    Tradeoff: Owns the audience instead of renting it from search; adds a privacy notice and a provider.
  • C. Publish a 'notify me when a new guide ships' link that opens a mail draft.
    Tradeoff: Zero infrastructure and honest; very low conversion.

Session recommendation: B only once there are at least three guides worth returning for. Before that it is asking for an email with nothing to send.

S-07 · Imagery

Current: A

  • A. Keep editorial illustration, clearly labelled as not the reviewed unit (current).
    Tradeoff: Consistent look, no licensing exposure, no misleading product photography.
  • B. Use Amazon-hosted product images through the approved image service.
    Tradeoff: Shows the actual product; images must be served by Amazon's tools and can change without notice.
  • C. Commission real photography of the units.
    Tradeoff: Highest trust and highest cost; needs physical units.

Session recommendation: B for the product hero on each product page, A everywhere else. Readers want to see the actual thing they are buying.

S-08 · The byline

Current: A

  • A. Keep the team byline, flagged as a weakness (current).
    Tradeoff: Honest and unchanging; weaker trust signal than a person.
  • B. Add a named editor with a short bio and a contact address.
    Tradeoff: Strongest trust signal; a person has to be accountable for the corrections.

Session recommendation: B as soon as a real person will take the byline. This is one of the cheapest trust wins available.

S-09 · Product scope

Current: A

  • A. Stay at five submitted products and go deep on them (current).
    Tradeoff: Focused and honest; a thin catalogue for a shopping site.
  • B. Add three to five comparison products per category, researched the same way.
    Tradeoff: Makes the categories credible; each added product needs its own source page and verification.
  • C. Expand into a second niche once the first converts.
    Tradeoff: Scales the model; splits attention before anything is proven.

Session recommendation: B, one category at a time, and only where a manufacturer page exists to verify against.

S-10 · Disclosure prominence

Current: A

  • A. Dark disclosure bar directly under the masthead, repeated in the footer (current).
    Tradeoff: Impossible to miss, and slightly heavy visually.
  • B. A lighter inline notice above the first product, plus the footer repeat.
    Tradeoff: Prettier; risks being read as decoration rather than disclosure.

Session recommendation: A. The FTC standard is that the disclosure must be hard to miss, and this one is.

S-11 · Design direction for the site

Current: A

  • A. Keep the warm editorial system as it stands: cream paper, terracotta accent, serif headlines, illustration-led cards, one navigation group.
    Tradeoff: It is the only direction that has actually been built and checked against the publishing gate, so it is the cheapest to keep and the safest to keep shipping.
  • B. Push it toward a shop: bigger product photography, price-shaped cards, a persistent comparison bar, and the guides demoted below the fold.
    Tradeoff: Closer to how a store reads, and further from why people trust the site. It also collides with the price and availability rule, which only allows prices that come from Amazon's own feeds.
  • C. Push it toward a reference work: dense specification tables, numbered source citations on every claim, and the front page replaced by an index.
    Tradeoff: Maximum authority and minimum charm. It reads like a database and converts like one.
  • D. Seasonal campaign layers: the same system with a monthly theme - roast season, grill season, small-kitchen season - carried by the hero band and the guide covers.
    Tradeoff: Gives the front page a reason to change and gives search engines a freshness signal, at the cost of a design change every month and the risk of looking like a banner ad.

Session recommendation: A as the base, with D layered on in the way the front page already hints at. The current system passes the publishing gate, reads like a publication, and carries the disclosure without shouting. If one thing changes, make it D: a seasonal band is the cheapest way to keep a buying guide looking alive without inventing facts.

S-12 · Which product leads the front page

Current: A

  • A. Lead with the shelf in register order: the dual-basket fryer, the french-door oven, the 8 qt fryer, the two thermometers (current).
    Tradeoff: Neutral and predictable. It also leads with the product whose listing and maker page disagree most, because that row is first for historical reasons.
  • B. Lead with the unit that has the most complete, least contradictory record.
    Tradeoff: The most defensible page becomes the most boring page, and the two units with the most interesting caveats get buried.
  • C. Lead with the seasonal unit and rotate the lead with the season.
    Tradeoff: Matches what people are actually shopping for at the time, and makes the front page change without anything false being claimed.

Session recommendation: C, with the caveat that the seasonal choice is a judgement about what people are buying, not a claim about what sells. No conversion data exists yet, so a rotation decided by preference and revisited when the click counter is live is the honest version.

S-13 · How opinionated the guides should be

Current: A

  • A. Stay descriptive and sourced: explain what each shape does, what the maker states, and what could not be confirmed (current).
    Tradeoff: Safe and durable, but it reads more like a well-organised manual than a magazine, and it leaves the reader to do the final comparison.
  • B. Take a position in each guide - say which type suits which household - while keeping every factual sentence sourced.
    Tradeoff: Better for the reader and better for search, at the cost of a stated opinion that has to be defensible without ever crossing into an unqualified product claim.
  • C. Add a clear recommendation per guide: one product we would buy, with the reason.
    Tradeoff: The strongest commercial move and the hardest to keep honest, because the site has never used a product. A recommendation without hands-on use is a recommendation from a specification sheet.

Session recommendation: B. Position is the one thing a specification sheet cannot supply, and every position in these guides is about suitability for a household rather than a claim about a product. C is not available yet: the site states plainly that it is not a test kitchen, and publishing a pick would contradict that in the same sentence.

S-14 · Article length and shape

Current: A

  • A. Medium guides of roughly 700 to 1,000 words, one question each, with a comparison table and a short reader-questions section (current).
    Tradeoff: Enough to be useful, short enough to be finished, and each one has to earn its place with a source.
  • B. Long-form guides of 1,500 words or more, with history, science and technique, as a magazine would run.
    Tradeoff: Stronger search performance and a real reason to subscribe, and a much larger surface for an error to hide in.
  • C. Short answer pieces of 300 words, designed to be skimmed and shared.
    Tradeoff: Fast to produce and easy to read, and unlikely to rank for the questions that actually matter.

Session recommendation: A for the running rhythm, with one B-length flagship per quarter. Depth is what a question-shaped guide earns by answering the whole question, not by hitting a word count; the current guides already show the shape works.

S-15 · Whether the shelf grows past five products

Current: A

  • A. Stay at the five products the owner supplied, and go deeper on them (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing is published that nobody checked, and the shelf stays too thin to rank for most category queries.
  • B. Add a second shelf of products from the same three categories, each with the same product-intent and observed-redirect discipline.
    Tradeoff: Doubles the pages that can rank, and doubles the verification load, including the link-intake work that is currently the bottleneck.
  • C. Add a 'considered alternatives' section naming types and models without linking them.
    Tradeoff: Cheap, and it cannot earn a commission, so it only pays if the guide itself earns the click.

Session recommendation: B, but only after Q-01 and Q-02 are closed. More products do not fix an unresolved link test; they multiply it. Until a Special Link is confirmed to credit a sale, a larger shelf is a larger untested surface.

S-16 · How the guides earn their traffic

Current: A

  • A. Question-shaped titles that read like a buyer asked them, each with the comparison table near the top (current).
    Tradeoff: Matches how people search and how they read, and it will never be as dense with keywords as a title written for a crawler.
  • B. Keyword-shaped titles with the question as a subtitle.
    Tradeoff: Usually more search volume, at the price of the tone that makes the site feel like it was written for someone rather than for a robot.
  • C. Keep question-shaped titles and add an internal 'questions we answer' index page that groups every guide by the question it answers.
    Tradeoff: One more page to maintain, and it gives a crawler a second, denser route through the same material.

Session recommendation: A with C. The titles are the reason the guides read like advice rather than filler, and an index page by question is a small addition that helps a reader and a crawler at once.

S-17 · Whether the evidence surfaces move up from the footer

Current: A

  • A. Keep the source register, questions, suggestions and flags in the footer and behind one 'Research desk' group in the navigation (current).
    Tradeoff: The reading experience stays clean, and a reader who never looks for the evidence never finds it.
  • B. Add a visible 'How we check' link in the masthead next to the navigation.
    Tradeoff: One more item in the top bar, and a stronger trust signal for the people who are checking.
  • C. Surface a short 'what we could not verify' line on every product card, not only on the product page.
    Tradeoff: More honesty in the place where the decision is made, and a busier card that some readers will read as a problem.

Session recommendation: B, and C for the two rows that have real caveats. The evidence pages exist and are well made; the only thing missing is a signpost that costs one item in the masthead.

S-18 · Distribution beyond the site itself

Current: A

  • A. Publish and wait: the sitemap, the feed and Search Console, and nothing else (current).
    Tradeoff: Cheapest, and the slowest possible route to traffic.
  • B. Republish each guide as one social post in a single channel, with the link back to the guide.
    Tradeoff: Real distribution, and a channel that may not be the one the reader is on.
  • C. Hold distribution until the click counter is live, so that distribution and measurement are switched on together.
    Tradeoff: Delays traffic by as long as the collector takes to deploy, and avoids sending people to pages whose performance cannot be read.

Session recommendation: C, then B. The measurement question is the one the brief puts first, and there is no point driving traffic to a page whose clicks cannot be counted. Once the collector is deployed, one channel is enough to test the guides; two channels at once would make it impossible to tell which one worked.
