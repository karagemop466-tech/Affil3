Decision board

Choose a path before anything changes

Decision queue: 'Current' describes the live setting; recommendations are proposals, not owner approvals. Optional changes remain unimplemented until chosen. The G-02 purchase-link hold is a safety gate and cannot be bypassed without recorded validation. Reply with a suggestion ID and option letter (for example 'S-03 B').

S-01 Two publications in one repository current: A

  • A. Keep the earlier /editorial/ edition live but excluded from search, and index only the root site (current). Trade-off: Nothing is lost and there is one indexed site; the old edition is still public if someone has the URL.
  • B. Retire /editorial/ entirely and redirect its URLs to the equivalent root pages. Trade-off: Cleanest signal for search engines; a redirect map has to be written and tested first.
  • C. Merge the better parts of the old edition into the root site and remove the duplicate pages. Trade-off: Best content outcome, most work, and the old edition's data conflicts with the root data and must be reconciled.

Our recommendation: B once the redirect map is written. Two editions of a five-page site is more surface than the content justifies.

S-02 Buy buttons while links are untested current: B

  • A. Keep buy buttons while the Associates test is pending (not selected). Trade-off: The site can earn from day one, but an untested link that silently fails credits nothing.
  • B. Hide every buy button until gate G-02 passes (current). Trade-off: Maximum compliance confidence; the site earns nothing until the owner tests.

Our recommendation: B until every product has a recorded Associates Central test, confirmed destination, and tracking-report evidence.

S-03 Where the analytics collector runs current: D

  • A. Run tools/collector.mjs on a small VPS the owner controls, behind HTTPS. Trade-off: Full control of raw data; the owner runs a server and patches it.
  • B. Run it as a serverless function on a platform that supports Node. Trade-off: No server to patch; cold starts and platform limits on storage.
  • C. Skip self-hosting and use a privacy-focused hosted analytics service. Trade-off: Fastest to start; one more third party, and the data leaves your control.
  • D. Stay inactive until there is traffic worth measuring (current). Trade-off: Zero maintenance; decisions stay on instinct, which is exactly what the brief asks to avoid.

Our recommendation: B. It removes the server maintenance objection without handing the data away.

S-04 Pricing display current: A

  • A. Show no prices; every button reads 'Check the current price on Amazon' (current). Trade-off: Always accurate, and compliant with the price clause; readers have to click to know a budget.
  • B. Enroll in the Creators API and display Amazon-served prices with a timestamp. Trade-off: Better for budget-led shoppers; more engineering, and a stale price is a compliance risk.
  • C. Show a coarse price band instead of a figure. Trade-off: Useful and stable; still a claim that needs checking, and it ages badly.

Our recommendation: A until traffic justifies the Creators API work. Then B.

S-05 Publishing cadence current: A

  • A. One new guide every two weeks (current). Trade-off: Sustainable; slow to build a topic cluster.
  • B. One new guide per week. Trade-off: Faster topical coverage; needs a real writing slot weekly.
  • C. A batch of eight guides before launch, then monthly. Trade-off: Reaches a useful site size quickly; risks thin pages if the batch is rushed.

Our recommendation: A, at a quality bar high enough that every guide earns its place. Ten thin guides are worse than four good ones.

S-06 Reader capture current: A

  • A. No email capture yet; the RSS feed is the only subscription route (current). Trade-off: Nothing to maintain and no consent obligations; no direct channel to readers.
  • B. Add a single-field email capture with double opt-in on guide pages. Trade-off: Owns the audience instead of renting it from search; adds a privacy notice and a provider.
  • C. Publish a 'notify me when a new guide ships' link that opens a mail draft. Trade-off: Zero infrastructure and honest; very low conversion.

Our recommendation: B only once there are at least three guides worth returning for. Before that it is asking for an email with nothing to send.

S-07 Imagery current: A

  • A. Keep editorial illustration, clearly labelled as not the reviewed unit (current). Trade-off: Consistent look, no licensing exposure, no misleading product photography.
  • B. Use Amazon-hosted product images through the approved image service. Trade-off: Shows the actual product; images must be served by Amazon's tools and can change without notice.
  • C. Commission real photography of the units. Trade-off: Highest trust and highest cost; needs physical units.

Our recommendation: B for the product hero on each product page, A everywhere else. Readers want to see the actual thing they are buying.

S-08 The byline current: A

  • A. Keep the team byline, flagged as a weakness (current). Trade-off: Honest and unchanging; weaker trust signal than a person.
  • B. Add a named editor with a short bio and a contact address. Trade-off: Strongest trust signal; a person has to be accountable for the corrections.

Our recommendation: B as soon as a real person will take the byline. This is one of the cheapest trust wins available.

S-09 Product scope current: A

  • A. Stay at five submitted products and go deep on them (current). Trade-off: Focused and honest; a thin catalogue for a shopping site.
  • B. Add three to five comparison products per category, researched the same way. Trade-off: Makes the categories credible; each added product needs its own source page and verification.
  • C. Expand into a second niche once the first converts. Trade-off: Scales the model; splits attention before anything is proven.

Our recommendation: B, one category at a time, and only where a manufacturer page exists to verify against.

S-10 Disclosure prominence current: A

  • A. Dark disclosure bar directly under the masthead, repeated in the footer (current). Trade-off: Impossible to miss, and slightly heavy visually.
  • B. A lighter inline notice above the first product, plus the footer repeat. Trade-off: Prettier; risks being read as decoration rather than disclosure.

Our recommendation: A. The FTC standard is that the disclosure must be hard to miss, and this one is.

S-11 AI research and publishing automation current: A

  • A. Keep research and publishing manual for now; use explicit source, validation, and disclosure gates (current). Trade-off: No provider cost or generated claims, but no automated research or drafts.
  • B. Pilot AI-assisted drafts using only approved product/query data, with citations and mandatory human approval. Trade-off: Can speed research and first drafts; requires an authorized source, model/provider, data terms, budget, and named editor.
  • C. Automatically publish AI-generated product pages and links. Trade-off: Highest automation and risk; requires validated source data, policy checks, working analytics, and rollback owner. Not recommended now.

Our recommendation: B only after Q-14 and D-09 are answered; do not automate publication before evidence and review gates exist.

To decide, reply with the suggestion ID and the letter, for example “S-03 B”. The site keeps working with whatever is current until then.
