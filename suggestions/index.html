Decision board

Suggestions awaiting a choice

Every item here is a decision waiting on the owner. Nothing is implemented until an option is chosen; the site keeps working with whichever option is marked current. Reply with the suggestion ID and the letter, for example 'S-03 B'.

S-01 · Two publications in one repository

Current: A

  • A. Keep the earlier /editorial/ edition live but excluded from search, and index only the root site (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing is lost and there is one indexed site; the old edition is still public if someone has the URL.
  • B. Retire /editorial/ entirely and redirect its URLs to the equivalent root pages.
    Tradeoff: Cleanest signal for search engines; a redirect map has to be written and tested first.
  • C. Merge the better parts of the old edition into the root site and remove the duplicate pages.
    Tradeoff: Best content outcome, most work, and the old edition's data conflicts with the root data and must be reconciled.

Session recommendation: B once the redirect map is written. Two editions of a five-page site is more surface than the content justifies.

S-02 · Buy buttons while links are untested

Current: A

  • A. Keep the buy buttons, labelled as provisional and pending a tracking check (current).
    Tradeoff: The site can earn from day one, but an untested link that silently fails credits nothing.
  • B. Hide every buy button until gate G-02 passes.
    Tradeoff: Maximum compliance confidence; the site earns nothing until the owner tests.

Session recommendation: A, but test within the first week. A page that cannot be clicked is a brochure.

S-03 · Where the analytics collector runs

Current: D

  • A. Run tools/collector.mjs on a small VPS the owner controls, behind HTTPS.
    Tradeoff: Full control of raw data; the owner runs a server and patches it.
  • B. Run it as a serverless function on a platform that supports Node.
    Tradeoff: No server to patch; cold starts and platform limits on storage.
  • C. Skip self-hosting and use a privacy-focused hosted analytics service.
    Tradeoff: Fastest to start; one more third party, and the data leaves your control.
  • D. Stay inactive until there is traffic worth measuring (current).
    Tradeoff: Zero maintenance; decisions stay on instinct, which is exactly what the brief asks to avoid.

Session recommendation: B. It removes the server maintenance objection without handing the data away.

S-04 · Pricing display

Current: A

  • A. Show no prices; every button reads 'Check the current price on Amazon' (current).
    Tradeoff: Always accurate, and compliant with the price clause; readers have to click to know a budget.
  • B. Enroll in the Creators API and display Amazon-served prices with a timestamp.
    Tradeoff: Better for budget-led shoppers; more engineering, and a stale price is a compliance risk.
  • C. Show a coarse price band instead of a figure.
    Tradeoff: Useful and stable; still a claim that needs checking, and it ages badly.

Session recommendation: A until traffic justifies the Creators API work. Then B.

S-05 · Publishing cadence

Current: A

  • A. One new guide every two weeks (current).
    Tradeoff: Sustainable; slow to build a topic cluster.
  • B. One new guide per week.
    Tradeoff: Faster topical coverage; needs a real writing slot weekly.
  • C. A batch of eight guides before launch, then monthly.
    Tradeoff: Reaches a useful site size quickly; risks thin pages if the batch is rushed.

Session recommendation: A, at a quality bar high enough that every guide earns its place. Ten thin guides are worse than four good ones.

S-06 · Reader capture

Current: A

  • A. No email capture yet; the RSS feed is the only subscription route (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing to maintain and no consent obligations; no direct channel to readers.
  • B. Add a single-field email capture with double opt-in on guide pages.
    Tradeoff: Owns the audience instead of renting it from search; adds a privacy notice and a provider.
  • C. Publish a 'notify me when a new guide ships' link that opens a mail draft.
    Tradeoff: Zero infrastructure and honest; very low conversion.

Session recommendation: B only once there are at least three guides worth returning for. Before that it is asking for an email with nothing to send.

S-07 · Imagery

Current: A

  • A. Keep editorial illustration, clearly labelled as not the reviewed unit (current).
    Tradeoff: Consistent look, no licensing exposure, no misleading product photography.
  • B. Use Amazon-hosted product images through the approved image service.
    Tradeoff: Shows the actual product; images must be served by Amazon's tools and can change without notice.
  • C. Commission real photography of the units.
    Tradeoff: Highest trust and highest cost; needs physical units.

Session recommendation: B for the product hero on each product page, A everywhere else. Readers want to see the actual thing they are buying.

S-08 · The byline

Current: A

  • A. Keep the team byline, flagged as a weakness (current).
    Tradeoff: Honest and unchanging; weaker trust signal than a person.
  • B. Add a named editor with a short bio and a contact address.
    Tradeoff: Strongest trust signal; a person has to be accountable for the corrections.

Session recommendation: B as soon as a real person will take the byline. This is one of the cheapest trust wins available.

S-09 · Product scope

Current: A

  • A. Stay at five submitted products and go deep on them (current).
    Tradeoff: Focused and honest; a thin catalogue for a shopping site.
  • B. Add three to five comparison products per category, researched the same way.
    Tradeoff: Makes the categories credible; each added product needs its own source page and verification.
  • C. Expand into a second niche once the first converts.
    Tradeoff: Scales the model; splits attention before anything is proven.

Session recommendation: B, one category at a time, and only where a manufacturer page exists to verify against.

S-10 · Disclosure prominence

Current: A

  • A. Dark disclosure bar directly under the masthead, repeated in the footer (current).
    Tradeoff: Impossible to miss, and slightly heavy visually.
  • B. A lighter inline notice above the first product, plus the footer repeat.
    Tradeoff: Prettier; risks being read as decoration rather than disclosure.

Session recommendation: A. The FTC standard is that the disclosure must be hard to miss, and this one is.

SG-27 · Reader-facing identity: one brand, imagery that is what it says it is

Current: A

  • A. Keep the two working names and keep editorial illustrations labelled as illustrations (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing is misrepresented; a reader moving between surfaces sees two names.
  • B. Pick one consumer name and domain, and commission original photography of the units you own.
    Tradeoff: Stronger recall and trust; needs a domain and physical units. Marketplace images remain Program Content and cannot simply be reused.

Session recommendation: B once a name and units exist. Until then A is the only honest option, because the illustrations are labelled as illustrations.

SG-28 · Reader-facing authorship: a name and a check date

Current: A

  • A. Team byline with a visible specification-check date on every guide and product page (current).
    Tradeoff: No invented person; a team byline is a weaker trust signal than a name.
  • B. Named editor with a short bio, a contact address and a stated review cadence.
    Tradeoff: The cheapest trust win available, but a real person has to answer for corrections.

Session recommendation: B as soon as a real person will take the byline. The check dates already exist, so this is a change of name, not of method.

SG-29 · Reader-facing findability: Search Console, topic clusters, RSS

Current: A

  • A. Ship canonical URLs, JSON-LD, sitemap, robots.txt and the RSS feed, and leave Search Console unverified (current).
    Tradeoff: Everything a crawler needs is present, but nobody can see which guides are being found.
  • B. Verify the Search Console property, submit the sitemap, and choose the next guide from the query report.
    Tradeoff: Turns topic choice from instinct into evidence; needs an owner Google account.

Session recommendation: B. Cheapest remaining decision, and the only one that shows whether the guides are read at all.

SG-30 · Reader-facing capture: RSS now, email only when there is something to send

Current: A

  • A. RSS only; no email capture and no seasonal hub until query data justifies one (current).
    Tradeoff: Nothing to maintain, no consent or retention obligations, and no direct channel to readers.
  • B. Add a double opt-in list and a seasonal hub once demand is measured, after reading the Amazon Trademark Guidelines on email.
    Tradeoff: Owns the audience instead of renting it from search; adds a privacy notice, a provider and an email-rules review.

Session recommendation: A until there are at least three guides worth returning for and the email rules have been read.
