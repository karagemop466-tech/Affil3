2. Reader trust and publication polish

The rows that decide whether this reads as something a person published: a byline, pictures, navigation in reader vocabulary, and a house style that can be checked rather than hoped for.

S-07 Imagery current: A awaiting your choice

  • A. Keep editorial illustration, clearly labelled as not the reviewed unit (current). current Trade-off: Consistent look, no licensing exposure, no misleading product photography.

S-11 Navigation labels current: A awaiting your choice

The masthead and footer are written in the project's own vocabulary. A reader who wants the disclosure, or the reason a product has no buy button, has to guess which footer entry carries it. Labels like 'Shop' work; labels that name our artefacts do not.

  • A. Keep the current labels and one flat footer list. current Trade-off: No work. The footer reads as a file listing rather than a set of places to go. · Effort: none · Risk: low
  • B. Relabel to reader vocabulary and split the footer into two headed groups: 'For readers' and 'Records and verification'. Every page stays reachable; only the words and the grouping change. Trade-off: About an hour in the generator. The gate already asserts every dashboard is footer-reachable, so nothing can be lost silently. · Effort: 1 hour · Risk: low
  • C. Cut the masthead to three items and move every record page behind a single 'How we review' page. Trade-off: Cleanest for a buyer, and /how-we-choose/ already exists to host it. Adds a click to reach the register, which the brief wants kept reachable. · Effort: 2 hours · Risk: medium

Our recommendation: B now, C once there is enough editorial content to carry it. B satisfies 'one navigation group' and 'dashboards reachable from the footer' while stopping the footer reading like a directory listing.

3. Findability

One publication for a crawler to index, a sitemap and feed that grow with each guide, and a way to see which queries actually arrive. Without the last of those, the next topic is chosen by instinct.

S-01 Two publications in one repository current: A awaiting your choice

  • A. Keep the earlier /editorial/ edition live but excluded from search, and index only the root site (current). current Trade-off: Nothing is lost and there is one indexed site; the old edition is still public if someone has the URL.
  • B. Retire /editorial/ entirely and redirect its URLs to the equivalent root pages. Trade-off: Cleanest signal for search engines; a redirect map has to be written and tested first.
  • C. Merge the better parts of the old edition into the root site and remove the duplicate pages. Trade-off: Best content outcome, most work, and the old edition's data conflicts with the root data and must be reconciled.

Our recommendation: B once the redirect map is written. Two editions of a five-page site is more surface than the content justifies.

4. Measurement

Click and conversion counting, the ledger, and reader capture. Deliberately after findability: measuring a page nobody can find produces zeros, and zeros are not a signal.

S-03 Where the analytics collector runs current: D awaiting your choice

  • A. Run tools/collector.mjs on a small VPS the owner controls, behind HTTPS. Trade-off: Full control of raw data; the owner runs a server and patches it.
  • B. Run it as a serverless function on a platform that supports Node. Trade-off: No server to patch; cold starts and platform limits on storage.
  • C. Skip self-hosting and use a privacy-focused hosted analytics service. Trade-off: Fastest to start; one more third party, and the data leaves your control.
  • D. Stay inactive until there is traffic worth measuring (current). current Trade-off: Zero maintenance; decisions stay on instinct, which is exactly what the brief asks to avoid.

Our recommendation: B. It removes the server maintenance objection without handing the data away.

S-18 Should the gate tell a product link apart from a citation? current: A awaiting your choice

The root gate marks every amazon.com anchor rel="sponsored". For the links that pay for this site that is exactly what Google's guidance asks for. For a citation to Amazon's own help or privacy pages it is a misstatement, because Google reserves sponsored for advertisements and paid placements and says a regular link needs no rel at all. The editorial publication reached the opposite answer and marks its citations class="cite" rel="nofollow", enforced by its own gate. Both are defensible; they are not the same policy, and this session hit the difference while writing /privacy/.

  • A. Keep the blanket rule and cite Amazon help pages as plain text rather than linking them (what /privacy/ does now). current Trade-off: Impossible to ship an unmarked product link, which is the failure that matters. It costs a few convenience links, and a plain-text URL is worse on a phone. · Effort: none · Risk: low
  • B. Make the rule precise and fail-closed: a link is product-intent if it targets /dp/, carries tag= or carries data-outbound, and must then be both sponsored and tagged; any other amazon.com link must not be sponsored. Anything that cannot be classified fails the build. Trade-off: Semantically correct against Google's guidance and it restores citation links. It is a change to a compliance gate, so it needs a negative test written first and should not be made by a build session on its own authority. · Effort: 2 hours · Risk: medium
  • C. Keep the blanket rule for product links and allow a short explicit allowlist of informational Amazon paths, checked literally. Trade-off: Narrower than B and easier to audit, because the allowlist is short and visible. It grows by hand and someone will eventually add a path they should not have. · Effort: 1 hour · Risk: low

Our recommendation: B, with the negative test written first: break a product link's tag, confirm the build fails, then make the change. Do not adopt B without that test, because the blanket rule is currently one of the things standing between this site and an untagged outbound link.

6. Platform and repo machinery

Registers, gates and the failure modes that produced real errors in this repository's own history. Cheap to fix, and they are what stops the same mistake recurring.

S-14 Derive every count on a page from the rows that page counts current: A awaiting your choice

This repository has shipped the same class of bug four times: a shop page saying 'four short links resolved' above a table of five; a resolved flag rendered as open; a source row naming three ASINs after the data said five; an empty category page under a heading that promised an explanation. Each time a sentence was written once by hand and never re-derived from the data it described. Two knowns in the questions register were superseded the same way and are now marked rather than deleted.

  • A. Fix each instance when it is found, which is what has happened so far. current Trade-off: Works until the next one. Nothing prevents recurrence, and the instances have been found by later sessions rather than by the build. · Effort: none · Risk: high
  • B. Add a gate rule banning hand-written count phrases in templates, and require the computed form instead. Trade-off: A few hours. Catches the class rather than the instance. · Effort: half a day · Risk: low
  • C. Add a build assertion that no rendered section has a non-empty heading over an empty body, and that every collection rendered on a page is non-empty unless the page says so. Trade-off: Cheaper than B and it also catches the empty-page case. · Effort: 2 hours · Risk: low

Our recommendation: B and C together. They are the only rows on this page that prevent a defect rather than describe one, and both are small. CI now catches a stale generated file, which is adjacent but different: it cannot see a sentence that was wrong the first time it was written.
