How to read this board

Read the tracks in order. Track 1 is owner-access work measured in minutes and it gates everything else: until one tagged link has been tested against a tracking report, the site cannot earn and no amount of design work changes that. Track 2 is the answer to 'it should not read like AI slop' and is deliberately concrete. Tracks 3 and 4 are findability and measurement. Track 5 records clauses found in this session's verbatim re-read that constrain what may be built at all. Track 6 is the machinery, and it is where this repository's own repeated bugs came from.

The tracks

These are the groups the rows fall into, in the order they unblock each other. Each card below names the track it belongs to.

  • 1. Unblock revenue — Nothing here earns until a tagged Special Link exists and has been tested once against the tracking report. These rows come first and are owner-access work measured in minutes, not build work.
  • 2. Reader trust and publication polish — The rows that decide whether this reads as something a person published: a byline, pictures, navigation in reader vocabulary, and a house style that can be checked rather than hoped for.
  • 3. Findability — One publication for a crawler to index, a sitemap and feed that grow with each guide, and a way to see which queries actually arrive. Without the last of those, the next topic is chosen by instinct.
  • 4. Measurement — Click and conversion counting, the ledger, and reader capture. Deliberately after findability: measuring a page nobody can find produces zeros, and zeros are not a signal.
  • 5. Compliance gates — Rules enforced by the build rather than remembered by an editor. Three clauses read verbatim in this session's re-fetch had never been recorded, and two of them change what may be built at all.
  • 6. Platform and repo machinery — Registers, gates and the failure modes that produced real errors in this repository's own history. Cheap to fix, and they are what stops the same mistake recurring.

    S-01 · Two publications in one repository

    Current: A

    • A. Keep the earlier /editorial/ edition live but excluded from search, and index only the root site (current).
      Tradeoff: Nothing is lost and there is one indexed site; the old edition is still public if someone has the URL.
    • B. Retire /editorial/ entirely and redirect its URLs to the equivalent root pages.
      Tradeoff: Cleanest signal for search engines; a redirect map has to be written and tested first.
    • C. Merge the better parts of the old edition into the root site and remove the duplicate pages.
      Tradeoff: Best content outcome, most work, and the old edition's data conflicts with the root data and must be reconciled.

    Session recommendation: B once the redirect map is written. Two editions of a five-page site is more surface than the content justifies.

    S-02 · Buy buttons while links are untested

    Current: A

    • A. Keep the buy buttons, labelled as provisional and pending a tracking check (current).
      Tradeoff: The site can earn from day one, but an untested link that silently fails credits nothing.
    • B. Hide every buy button until gate G-02 passes.
      Tradeoff: Maximum compliance confidence; the site earns nothing until the owner tests.

    Session recommendation: A, but test within the first week. A page that cannot be clicked is a brochure.

    S-03 · Where the analytics collector runs

    Current: D

    • A. Run tools/collector.mjs on a small VPS the owner controls, behind HTTPS.
      Tradeoff: Full control of raw data; the owner runs a server and patches it.
    • B. Run it as a serverless function on a platform that supports Node.
      Tradeoff: No server to patch; cold starts and platform limits on storage.
    • C. Skip self-hosting and use a privacy-focused hosted analytics service.
      Tradeoff: Fastest to start; one more third party, and the data leaves your control.
    • D. Stay inactive until there is traffic worth measuring (current).
      Tradeoff: Zero maintenance; decisions stay on instinct, which is exactly what the brief asks to avoid.

    Session recommendation: B. It removes the server maintenance objection without handing the data away.

    S-04 · Pricing display

    Current: A

    • A. Show no prices; every button reads 'Check the current price on Amazon' (current).
      Tradeoff: Always accurate, and compliant with the price clause; readers have to click to know a budget.
    • B. Enroll in the Creators API and display Amazon-served prices with a timestamp.
      Tradeoff: Better for budget-led shoppers; more engineering, and a stale price is a compliance risk.
    • C. Show a coarse price band instead of a figure.
      Tradeoff: Useful and stable; still a claim that needs checking, and it ages badly.

    Session recommendation: A until traffic justifies the Creators API work. Then B.

    S-05 · Publishing cadence

    Current: A

    • A. One new guide every two weeks (current).
      Tradeoff: Sustainable; slow to build a topic cluster.
    • B. One new guide per week.
      Tradeoff: Faster topical coverage; needs a real writing slot weekly.
    • C. A batch of eight guides before launch, then monthly.
      Tradeoff: Reaches a useful site size quickly; risks thin pages if the batch is rushed.

    Session recommendation: A, at a quality bar high enough that every guide earns its place. Ten thin guides are worse than four good ones.

    S-06 · Reader capture

    Current: A

    • A. No email capture yet; the RSS feed is the only subscription route (current).
      Tradeoff: Nothing to maintain and no consent obligations; no direct channel to readers.
    • B. Add a single-field email capture with double opt-in on guide pages.
      Tradeoff: Owns the audience instead of renting it from search; adds a privacy notice and a provider.
    • C. Publish a 'notify me when a new guide ships' link that opens a mail draft.
      Tradeoff: Zero infrastructure and honest; very low conversion.

    Session recommendation: B only once there are at least three guides worth returning for. Before that it is asking for an email with nothing to send.

    S-07 · Imagery

    Current: A

    • A. Keep editorial illustration, clearly labelled as not the reviewed unit (current).
      Tradeoff: Consistent look, no licensing exposure, no misleading product photography.
    • B. Use Amazon-hosted product images through the approved image service.
      Tradeoff: Shows the actual product; images must be served by Amazon's tools and can change without notice.
    • C. Commission real photography of the units.
      Tradeoff: Highest trust and highest cost; needs physical units.

    Session recommendation: B for the product hero on each product page, A everywhere else. Readers want to see the actual thing they are buying.

    S-08 · The byline

    Current: A

    • A. Keep the team byline, flagged as a weakness (current).
      Tradeoff: Honest and unchanging; weaker trust signal than a person.
    • B. Add a named editor with a short bio and a contact address.
      Tradeoff: Strongest trust signal; a person has to be accountable for the corrections.

    Session recommendation: B as soon as a real person will take the byline. This is one of the cheapest trust wins available.

    S-09 · Product scope

    Current: A

    • A. Stay at five submitted products and go deep on them (current).
      Tradeoff: Focused and honest; a thin catalogue for a shopping site.
    • B. Add three to five comparison products per category, researched the same way.
      Tradeoff: Makes the categories credible; each added product needs its own source page and verification.
    • C. Expand into a second niche once the first converts.
      Tradeoff: Scales the model; splits attention before anything is proven.

    Session recommendation: B, one category at a time, and only where a manufacturer page exists to verify against.

    S-10 · Disclosure prominence

    Current: A

    • A. Dark disclosure bar directly under the masthead, repeated in the footer (current).
      Tradeoff: Impossible to miss, and slightly heavy visually.
    • B. A lighter inline notice above the first product, plus the footer repeat.
      Tradeoff: Prettier; risks being read as decoration rather than disclosure.

    Session recommendation: A. The FTC standard is that the disclosure must be hard to miss, and this one is.

    S-11 · Four editions on one domain

    Current: A

    • A. Index the root publication only; keep the other editions reachable but excluded from search, and list them on one archive page (current).
      Tradeoff: One site to rank, no content deleted, and each other edition keeps its own generator and gate if the owner later wants it on its own domain.
    • B. Publish the other editions on separate domains, each with its own tracking ID.
      Tradeoff: Cleanest separation, but it needs three domains, three Associates declarations and three link sets to maintain.
    • C. Unpublish the other editions and keep only their pages in the repository history.
      Tradeoff: The strongest single signal, and the least reversible: the URLs stop resolving and the archived editions become invisible to readers who had them.

    Session recommendation: A for now. One indexed site is what the brief asks for, nothing is deleted, and option B stays open because each edition is already a self-contained build.

    S-12 · Publishing the ThermoMaven buy button

    Current: A

    • A. Publish the buy button against the observed listing ASIN, with the model-correspondence caveat written on the product page (current).
      Tradeoff: The reader gets a working, tagged link to the exact unit the submitted link resolves to, and the honest caveat is one click away.
    • B. Keep the button hidden until the listing model field confirms the F1 Ace.
      Tradeoff: The tightest possible claim, at the cost of one of five products having no way to buy, which reads to a buyer as broken.
    • C. Point the button at the manufacturer F1 Ace page instead of the Amazon listing.
      Tradeoff: Breaks the affiliate link and the tracking the site exists for.

    Session recommendation: A. The product identity is confirmed by two independent observations and the feature match is exact; what is unconfirmed is a model name, which is a labelling question, not an identity question.

    S-13 · A side-by-side comparison page

    Current: A

    • A. Publish /compare/: one table of the five products on published specifications only, with a blank cell wherever a maker is silent (current).
      Tradeoff: It is the page a buyer actually wants and the strongest internal link target for every guide.
    • B. Keep the comparison inside the shop table and the category pages only.
      Tradeoff: Fewer pages to maintain, and buyers must assemble the comparison themselves from a twelve-column table.
    • C. Add comparison products beyond the five submitted rows so the table has more than one option per category.
      Tradeoff: More useful to a buyer, but each added product needs the same verification work and none of them has been submitted by the owner.

    Session recommendation: A now, and C later if Q-01 and Q-02 are closed and there is budget for real product research.

    S-14 · Derive every count on a page from the rows that page counts · 6. Platform and repo machinery

    Current: A

    • A. Fix each instance when it is found, which is what has happened so far.
      Tradeoff: Works until the next one. Nothing prevents recurrence, and the instances have been found by later sessions rather than by the build.
    • B. Add a gate rule banning hand-written count phrases in templates, and require the computed form instead.
      Tradeoff: A few hours. Catches the class rather than the instance.
    • C. Add a build assertion that no rendered section has a non-empty heading over an empty body, and that every collection rendered on a page is non-empty unless the page says so.
      Tradeoff: Cheaper than B and it also catches the empty-page case.

    Session recommendation: B and C together. They are the only rows on this page that prevent a defect rather than describe one, and both are small. CI now catches a stale generated file, which is adjacent but different: it cannot see a sentence that was wrong the first time it was written.

    S-15 · Record the two policy clauses that constrain the AI half of the brief · 5. Compliance gates

    Current: A

    • A. Treat the automation in the brief as unqualified and build it.
      Tradeoff: The fastest path to what the brief describes and the likeliest path to a material breach, which permits immediate termination under Operating Agreement §6.
    • B. Build only automation that touches no Special Link and no Program Content: internal registers, the build, the gate, the analytics pipeline, and drafts written from manufacturer pages with citations. Keep pricing analysis off the Site. Ask Amazon about §5 before going further.
      Tradeoff: Slower, and part of the brief stays unbuilt. Everything already in this repository is inside it.
    • C. Request PA API / Creators API access and Amazon's written approval, then build the full pipeline.
      Tradeoff: The only route to prices, ratings and the automated research the brief describes. Needs an approved account and a qualifying site, so it depends on S-02 and Q-03.

    Session recommendation: B now and C as the destination. Published on the compliance page in this session so the boundary is visible to whoever builds next rather than rediscovered later.

    S-16 · A privacy notice for the click counter · 5. Compliance gates

    Current: A

    • A. Ship no notice.
      Tradeoff: The counter sets no cookies and the endpoint is empty, so nothing is presently collected. The obligation to disclose the technology does not obviously disappear because the endpoint is unset.
    • B. Publish a short /privacy/ page stating what the counter does, what it does not collect, that the endpoint is unset, and what would change if a third-party analytics product were adopted.
      Tradeoff: An hour, and done in this session for review. It is a draft of a legal-adjacent page and should be read rather than trusted.
    • C. Adopt a hosted consent and privacy service.
      Tradeoff: Proportionate for a site with a real audience and third-party processors. Overkill now, and it adds a processor of its own.

    Session recommendation: B, implemented in this session as a draft. It closes Q-11 and answers part of Q-16; whether the wording is adequate where the site is operated from is a question for counsel, not something a build session should settle.

    S-17 · One product register or two · 6. Platform and repo machinery

    Current: A

    • A. Keep both and re-check them against each other each session (current).
      Tradeoff: No migration risk. The check is manual, and manual checks here have a documented record of being missed.
    • B. Add a gate rule that fails the build when the two registers disagree on any shared field for any product.
      Tradeoff: About an hour. It does not remove the duplication but it makes disagreement impossible to ship, which is the failure that actually happened.
    • C. Merge into one register with per-publication views.
      Tradeoff: Removes the duplication permanently. The largest data change available here, and it touches files two earlier sessions built.

    Session recommendation: B now, and C if S-01 chooses to consolidate the publications. B is a few dozen lines and converts a class of silent error into a build failure.

    S-18 · Should the gate tell a product link apart from a citation? · 5. Compliance gates

    Current: A

    • A. Keep the blanket rule and cite Amazon help pages as plain text rather than linking them (what /privacy/ does now).
      Tradeoff: Impossible to ship an unmarked product link, which is the failure that matters. It costs a few convenience links, and a plain-text URL is worse on a phone.
    • B. Make the rule precise and fail-closed: a link is product-intent if it targets /dp/, carries tag= or carries data-outbound, and must then be both sponsored and tagged; any other amazon.com link must not be sponsored. Anything that cannot be classified fails the build.
      Tradeoff: Semantically correct against Google's guidance and it restores citation links. It is a change to a compliance gate, so it needs a negative test written first and should not be made by a build session on its own authority.
    • C. Keep the blanket rule for product links and allow a short explicit allowlist of informational Amazon paths, checked literally.
      Tradeoff: Narrower than B and easier to audit, because the allowlist is short and visible. It grows by hand and someone will eventually add a path they should not have.

    Session recommendation: B, with the negative test written first: break a product link's tag, confirm the build fails, then make the change. Do not adopt B without that test, because the blanket rule is currently one of the things standing between this site and an untagged outbound link.

    S-19 · A house style rule that can be checked · 2. Reader trust and publication polish

    Current: A

    • A. Rely on editorial judgement per page.
      Tradeoff: Free, and it is how the current copy was produced - which is good enough that the diagnosis is worth writing down rather than repeating.
    • B. Publish a house style file with do/don't rules and real before-and-after examples from this site, and check the mechanical parts in the gate: banned opener phrases, headings that are not claims, uniform list-item length within a section.
      Tradeoff: Half a day. The gate catches the mechanical failures; the rest stays editorial. Publishing the standard does more for reader trust than any badge.
    • C. Add a readability-score threshold per page to the gate.
      Tradeoff: Objective and easy to implement, and it optimises for the wrong thing: it pushes copy toward short dumb sentences, which is its own kind of slop.

    Session recommendation: B. The forbidden list and the twelve prescriptions are already written at /design-brief/; what is missing is the gate rule and the decision to hold future copy to it.

    S-20 · Crawl control for a site served at a project path

    Current: A

    • A. Keep the root publication as the only indexable site, submit sitemap.xml by hand in Search Console, and leave the other editions as they are (current).
      Tradeoff: No change to the repository. The other editions stay publicly reachable and mostly without noindex, so duplicate copies can still surface in search.
    • B. Add a noindex meta tag to every non-root page in the repository (counter/, counterwise/, crisp-index/, legacy/, counter-desk/), then keep the root publication as the only indexed site.
      Tradeoff: Works without host-root access. It edits generated pages owned by earlier sessions, so it needs an owner decision and a rebuild of each surface.
    • C. Remove the other editions from the Pages site (archive them to a branch or a tag) so only one publication is served.
      Tradeoff: Strongest single-site outcome and simplest to reason about. Loses the public evidence pages that the archive page links to; Q-10 and S-11 apply.
    • D. Serve the root publication from a repository named for the owner's GitHub Pages host, so robots.txt is read from the host root.
      Tradeoff: Makes robots.txt work as written. Changes the public URL, so every link, the sitemap and any Search Console property must move.

    Session recommendation: A for now, plus the manual Search Console submission (which is owner work and is needed under every option). Then B if the other editions stay online, or C if the owner decides to retire them (Q-10). D only if the owner wants a clean host-root URL. The current state does not meet the 'one site to index' goal, and this suggestion does not claim that it does.
