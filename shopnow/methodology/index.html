How we decide what to publish

Four rules

1. A number needs a source, or it does not appear. Every figure on this site resolves to a row in the source register: an official page, fetched or excerpted, with an access date and the exact quote. Four of the eleven source rows are marked unresolved. That is not a gap in the work; it is the work telling you where it stopped.

2. We do not claim to have tested anything. There is no test kitchen behind this site. No product has been measured, weighed, cooked in, or disassembled by us. First-person testing claims, lab claims, and hands-on framing do not appear here, and a build check rejects them by name. When a guide says "check X", it means you should check X, not that we did.

3. Prices and ratings belong to Amazon, at the moment of purchase. Amazon's Program Policies permit a site to show price and availability only when the data comes from an Amazon-served link or its catalog API, with a 24-hour refresh ceiling. We have no API access - and the eligibility gate for it sits behind the sales that access would help produce. So we publish no monetary figure at all, and a build check fails if one slips in.

4. The sponsor relationship is stated where you are reading, not on a page you have to go find. The disclosure sentence sits under the masthead on every page and again in the footer, in text, not behind a button. That placement is deliberate: the FTC has said a hyperlinked "DISCLOSURE" control is not clear and conspicuous, because most people will not click it.

What a machine enforces

Compliance that depends on someone remembering is compliance that fails on a busy day. So the checks are code, they run on every build, and CI blocks the merge:

GateWhat it catches
Disclosure presentany page missing the required sentence above the fold or in the footer
Sponsored markingany commerce link without rel="sponsored nofollow" and a visible Sponsored label
Tracking tagany Amazon link whose tag= does not match the site's tracking ID
Price guardany currency pattern reaching the published site

Verification guarda field published as fact with no source row behind it
Link integrityany broken internal link, and any page missing from the sitemap or feed

The list is the file scripts/checks.mjs. If you want to argue with a rule, the argument is with a script, not with a person's judgement on a bad day.

How a product gets listed

A product is only published when its listing has been resolved: we follow the supplied link, record the destination, and store the intended identifier and the observed destination in separate fields so a reader can see which is which. Where a link would not resolve - as with one of our five - the product has no page and no card, and the register says UNRESOLVED.

We did not guess a brand, a category, or a price to fill that row. Five products came in; four are on the shop floor; one is in the dashboard with a blank where the name should be. Full ledger.

What "verified" means here, precisely

  • verbatim - we fetched the official page in this session and copied the text.
  • excerpt - official-domain text returned by a search index; the page itself would not load in this environment. Enough to publish a citation, not enough to quote at length.
  • unresolved - sources conflict, or the page could not be reached. Nothing was adopted. The row stays, with the conflict described.

No row on this site uses a fourth category. There is no "probably", and nothing is filled in because the blank looked bad.

Reader-facing honesty page

If we get a fact wrong, the register should show it before you have to email us. That is what Flagged for review is for: ten open items, each with the source link, the exact owner action that would close it, and what we withheld from the site as a result.
