OUR WORKING STANDARD · LAST CHECKED 20 NOV 2026

Useful, clear, and honest about what we know.

This is an editorial site and verification scaffold, not a live AI product-research or conversion-optimization platform. The repository initially contained no product records, research, analytics backend or editorial workflow. The submitted product category itself remains unverified; air-fryer topics are editorial hypotheses supplied in the request, not a finding about those links. This publication shell adds a cautious process and makes the missing evidence visible.

Product identity and link records

Every product-intent record keeps separate fields for the submitted URL, intended ASIN, official listing URL, confirmation date, confirmation source and observed redirect target. We do not populate an intended ASIN from a shortened path or treat a redirect as confirmation of intent. Observed destination and intended destination must never be merged.

The five strings in the submitted URLs are nine characters long as written. Amazon describes ASINs as unique 10-character alphanumeric identifiers. This mismatch flags the submitted strings for review; it does not establish that the short links are invalid, malicious or noncompliant.

Shopping links and disclosures

No product purchase link is activated on this site. Before activation, review the destination in the enrolled Amazon Associates workflow, verify the tracking ID and destination, and confirm attribution in authorized first-party reports. Each published outbound product link must be clearly disclosed nearby and marked with rel="sponsored" (plus appropriate security attributes).

The disclosure appears under the masthead and in the footer on each page. Amazon’s current Operating Agreement requires the Associates statement to appear clearly and prominently. Amazon guidance and FTC guidance also emphasize clear, conspicuous disclosure near an endorsement or link. This implementation is a design measure, not legal advice or a compliance guarantee.

Editorial evidence

Articles on the site are general buyer checklists. They do not claim product testing, expertise, performance, product superiority, search popularity, search volume, trend status, commission rates, conversion, or revenue. Model-specific details should come from official listings or manuals and be recorded with source and date. Editorial hypotheses are not presented as keyword research.

Analytics and campaign reporting

The five event names are page_view (page opened), guide_open (internal guide selected), outbound_click (a link explicitly marked as outbound), suggestion_vote (a local editorial choice), and reader_capture (a topic-interest toggle on this device). The browser script stores events locally in the visitor’s browser only. That is a development scaffold—not site-wide analytics. GitHub Pages is static hosting and does not run a server-side endpoint. No endpoint, Search Console property, query data or Amazon conversion report is connected. The campaign ledger is intentionally empty; reported orders and revenue stay blank until authorized first-party reports are available.

Publishing checks

  • Verify identity and official listing for each product; retain intended and observed destinations separately.
  • Test one Special Link per product within the enrolled Associates account, and verify destination plus tracking report.
  • Check visible page disclosure, near-link disclosure and sponsored rel for every outbound product link.
  • Use Amazon-authorized sources and follow current API/PA API terms; do not scrape retail pages or invent ratings.
  • Validate all new guide URLs in sitemap and RSS, then check rendered pages and broken links.
  • Review Search Console query reports before describing topics as popular or prioritizing on demand.
  • Use current official policies; obtain qualified legal/compliance review for questions about specific claims.

Official sources

  • Amazon Associates Program Operating Agreement — includes the required Associates statement and program conditions.
  • Amazon Associates Help: link-level disclosure — examples and placement guidance.
  • Amazon Associates Program Policies — incorporated policies, API/content and pricing requirements.
  • Amazon: ASIN overview — identifies ASIN as a unique 10-character alphanumeric code.
  • FTC: Endorsement Guides, what people are asking — disclosure guidance for endorsements and affiliate marketing.
  • FTC: Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers — clear and conspicuous disclosure guidance.
  • Google Search Central: qualify outbound links — use rel="sponsored" for paid placements and advertising links.
  • GitHub Pages documentation — static site publishing documentation.

Sources were reviewed on 20 November 2026. Affiliate program terms and policies can change; re-check them before launch and whenever link tooling or product content changes.
