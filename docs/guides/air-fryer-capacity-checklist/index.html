Air fryers Working title

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Capacity numbers in air fryer titles can mean total volume or one basket. A short checklist to work out which one you are reading.

By the Editorial team · Published 20 Nov 2026 · 2 min read

Working title. An editorial hypothesis, not a claim about popularity, search volume or product quality. We have not tested these products. Everything quoted comes from listing text or public guidance.

Start with the unit, not the number

A capacity figure is only useful once you know what it measures. In the listings we checked, three different kinds of wording appear in the titles:

  • Total volume, for example "6 Quart" in the first dual-basket listing (see listing) or "8 Qt" in the basket listing (see listing).
  • Per-basket volume, for example "2 Independent 3Qt" baskets in the same first listing.
  • Oven interior, for example "26QT" and "Extra Large Interior" in the French-door listing (see listing).

These are not interchangeable. A 6-quart total with two 3-quart baskets is not the same as one 6-quart basket. Ask which figure you are reading every time.

This guide is editorial. We have not tested these appliances. The points below are checks you can make yourself.

The three-meal test

  1. Write down the three meals you cook most often.
  2. For each one, name the pan or tray you would normally use.
  3. Ask: does that pan, at its normal depth, fit a basket or oven rack from the listing? Check the product details on the listing page for dimensions. Compare them with your pan, not with a number in the title.
  4. If a meal needs two pans, a dual-basket model may suit it. If it needs one large dish, check the rack or tray size, not the total quarts.

Four questions that cut through the number

  • Is the figure total or per basket? (See above.)
  • Does the listing include the accessories you would use? A title that says "Six Cooking Accessories Included" is a count, not a size.
  • Can the basket or tray come out and go in the dishwasher? Two titles in our set say "Dishwasher Safe" or "Dishwasher-Safe", but the manual decides which parts qualify.
  • Is the basket nonstick? The dual-basket and basket listings say "Nonstick" in the title. Check the manual's care section before you buy.

A checklist

  • I know whether the number I am reading is total or per basket
  • I have compared the tray or basket size with the pans I actually use
  • I have checked the accessories count against the accessories I need
  • I have checked the manual, not just the title, for dishwasher and nonstick claims
  • I have a note of the listing's return window

What this guide does not do

It does not say which capacity is right for you. It does not claim any model cooks more evenly than another. Listing titles are written by the seller or manufacturer. We have only read them.
