How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

Most kitchen appliances are returned — or resented — not because they cook poorly, but because they crowd the counter or punish you at cleanup. Here's how to weigh both before you buy.

Part one: counter space

  1. Measure where the appliance will live. Depth and width, plus clearance for hot air and the door swing.
  2. Read dimensions off the official listing, not the marketing copy. Example from our register: a 6-liter dual-basket air fryer lists 13.38"D × 11.81"W × 10.63"H.
  3. Be honest about permanence. An appliance that stays on the counter needs to earn that spot daily. One that lives in a cabinet needs to be light enough to move and cool enough to store.

Part two: cleanup

  1. Check what's dishwasher-safe on the listing. Many models advertise dishwasher-safe baskets and trays — confirm exactly which parts, and whether your machine's rack fits them.
  2. Count the parts you'll wash. A dual-basket model has more removable parts than a single-basket one. Fewer parts usually means faster cleanup.
  3. Consider nonstick care. Nonstick baskets are common; treat them well and they last, but they're usually the part you'll scrub most.

The trade-off, spelled out

Bigger capacity usually means more surface area to clean and a larger footprint. A dual-basket model doubles your cooking flexibility and doubles the baskets to wash. The "best" answer is the one where your cooking habits, your counter, and your cleanup tolerance all point the same way.
