Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Air fryers come in two shapes, and they suit different kitchens. Here's what to compare before you pick one — with the verified details from three listings currently in our register. Prices and ratings are as listed on 2026-11-20.

The two shapes, in plain terms

Basket-style air fryers cook in a removable basket, like a deep-fryer basket with hot air. They tend to be compact, fast to heat, and easy to wash. Oven-style air fryers look like small countertop convection ovens — often with a door, a larger cavity, and room for more food or accessories.

The right choice is rarely about which is "better." It's about what you actually cook, how much counter space you have, and how much cleaning you're willing to do.

What to compare

  1. Capacity in real terms. Check the total capacity and how it's split. A dual-basket model with two 3-quart baskets (6 quarts combined) lets you cook two things at once; a large single basket suits bigger loads of one thing.
  2. Footprint, not just capacity. A 6-liter dual-basket model measured 13.38"D × 11.81"W × 10.63"H on its listing — measure your counter before you buy.
  3. What you'll cook most. Frequent batch-frying of frozen foods favors baskets. Roasting, baking, or toasting for a family often favors the bigger oven-style cavity.
  4. Cleaning. Look for dishwasher-safe baskets and trays. Every model in our register advertises dishwasher-safe baskets — confirm what's actually washable in your machine.
  5. Temperature range and presets. Check the range you'll actually use (many go 200–450°F) and whether the presets match your cooking.

The short answer

If your kitchen is small and you mostly air-fry frozen foods in batches, a basket-style model is the practical pick. If you roast, bake, or cook for a larger group and want one machine to do more, an oven-style model earns its larger footprint. Measure your counter, check the return window, and buy from a listing you've verified — that's the whole game.
