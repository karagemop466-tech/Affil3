A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Capacity marketing gets confusing fast: quarts, liters, baskets, "family size." Here's a checklist that cuts through it — take it to any listing page.

1. Read the capacity the way a cook would

  • Find the number, then the unit. A listing may say "6 liters" or "8 quarts" — same idea, different unit. Note the unit so you can compare.
  • Check if capacity is per-basket or combined. A dual-basket model may advertise a combined total across two baskets.
  • Ask what fits, not just what's advertised. The practical question: how many servings can you cook in one go for your household?

2. Match capacity to your household

  • 1–2 people, batch-frying frozen foods: a compact basket model is usually plenty.
  • A family that cooks together: a dual-basket or larger oven-style cavity lets you cook two things at once.
  • Large loads, roasts, or baking: oven-style capacities in the 20+ quart range give you room.

3. Check the footprint before you fall for the capacity

  • Measure your counter space — depth, width, and the clearance you need for hot air circulation.
  • Look up dimensions on the listing. Example from our register: a 6-liter dual-basket model lists 13.38"D × 11.81"W × 10.63"H. If that won't fit where you plan to use it, the capacity number won't matter.

4. Verify before you buy

  • Confirm the capacity on the official listing — not in a review title or an ad.
  • Re-check the price and rating the day you buy. Both change; our figures are snapshots as listed on 2026-11-20.
