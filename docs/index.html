The counter-top desk

Buy the right shape. Skip the sales pitch.

We do not have a test kitchen, we have not cooked in these machines, and we will not pretend otherwise. What we do have is the list of numbers that actually decide a countertop purchase - and the discipline to show you which ones we could not confirm.

Start with the guides The products Why there are no prices

Read this first

Everything on this site is written for one moment: you, standing at a listing page, trying to work out whether this machine is right for your kitchen. Four guides cover the four decisions that moment contains - shape, capacity, cleanup and counter cost, and whether a multi-function claim is real. A fifth teaches you to read the listing itself in ninety seconds.

The two questions that decide almost everything

  1. Basket or cabinet? A shallow round basket you shake, or a door you open like an oven. Start here.
  2. How many people are you really cooking for, and will you wash it? Start with capacity and the counter test.

What we publish, and what we refuse to

We publish: the products, the exact official listing for each, the date we confirmed it, and the specific questions we think are worth your time.

We do not publish: prices, availability, star ratings, review counts, or any test we did not run. Amazon's terms let a site display price and availability only when Amazon supplies the data through its own link or its catalog API, and this project holds no API credentials - so rather than post a number that quietly rots, we post the question the number answers. Every withheld field is named, in the open, on Flagged for review.

That is the whole deal. It makes our pages less impressive and considerably more checkable. Here is how we decide what goes up.
