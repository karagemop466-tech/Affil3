Counter Tested is a small publishing operation with one shelf of products and a habit of showing the paperwork. We write buying questions and comparison methods for kitchen appliances, publish what we verified, and leave blank what we did not.

## What we do

- Publish guides shaped around the questions a buyer can answer for themselves — capacity in single layers, cleanup cost in parts and minutes, counter space in centimetres.
- Maintain a register of every factual assertion the site makes, each pointing at the official page we read it from, with the date.
- Run the site through a build gate that fails when the paperwork is missing: no disclosure, no deploy.

## What we do not do

- We do not display prices, star ratings or review quotes. Amazon permits those on our site only through a link they serve or data pulled under their API licence, and we have neither. It is also the sort of number that goes stale the day it is typed.
- We do not scrape or warehouse product data, and we do not train anything on it. Those uses are outside what the licence permits without prior written approval, so the automation here works on our own content instead.
- We do not say "we tested it" until somebody has.
- We do not take an order through our own links to check that tracking works. That is prohibited, so the link test is a destination check and a next-day report check, and the site waits.

## Why the shelf has gaps in it

Five product listings were handed to us as short links that we could not resolve from where we build this site. Rather than fill the gaps with plausible-looking text, we published the state of each one: what we were given, what we could not confirm, what a human has to click. The details are on the [product status page](product-status.html), and the unresolved items are on [Flagged for review](flagged-for-review.html).

If you are a reader, that should change how much you trust a blank cell here. If you are the person who owns this project, it is a to-do list with commands attached, in the repo.

## Money

Links to Amazon are affiliate links, marked as such in the page markup, and we may earn a commission on qualifying purchases. The commission comes from Amazon's side of the transaction; it does not change what a reader pays and it does not change what we are allowed to claim. There is a required sentence about this at the top of every page because the programme agreement says we have to print it, and near every link because the FTC's guidance says the top of a page alone is not enough.
