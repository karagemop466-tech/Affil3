Situation first

The same register, read the other way round: from what you are cooking to the row that fits.

DP-01

You cook two things that need different times

Two independent 3 qt baskets in one 6 qt body, with the maker's Sync Finish feature for getting them to the table together.

CHEFMAN 6 Qt. Dual Basket

The register records this unit as being for two foods on two timers, in the footprint of a single basket fryer.

The trade-off: Each 3 qt basket is smaller than one 6 qt drawer, so a single large batch gets split.

Sources: S11 · S04 observed listing pages, one per row

DP-02

You are cooking for a group and want it all at once

26 qt of cavity, french doors, and a multi-function convection oven - the largest cavity on this shelf.

Emeril Lagasse 26 QT French Door

The register records this unit as being for cooking a whole meal at once, on more than one rack.

The trade-off: It is a countertop oven, so it needs oven-sized counter depth and it is the largest footprint in this set.

Sources: S13 · S04 observed listing pages, one per row

DP-03

You want one big batch and no divider in the way

A single 8 qt basket, so a batch does not have to be split across two drawers.

Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt

The register records this unit as being for one big batch in one basket, with no divider to work around.

The trade-off: No window and no second basket, so checking food means opening the drawer. The listing and the maker's own page also describe two different 8 qt models - read the conflict note on the product page before ordering.

Sources: S12 · S16 · S04 observed listing pages, one per row

DP-04

You want to stop guessing when the meat is done

Two ways to do it: a handheld spot check at the grill, or a leave-in wireless probe that reports without you standing next to it.

CHEF iQ Sense or ThermoMaven instant-read

The register records one unit as a quick spot check rather than an unattended cook, and the other as knowing when to pull the meat without standing next to the grill.

The trade-off: The leave-in probe depends on an app and a hub, so a flat battery or a dropped connection is a cooking problem. The instant-read unit reads one spot at a time.

Note: ThermoMaven instant-read is listed here as context only; no submitted link resolved to it, so no buy button is published. FLG-001 FLG-025

Sources: S14 · S04 observed listing pages, one per row

DP-05

Your counter space is the real constraint

Depth as each manufacturer states it: 10.63 in for the dual-basket fryer, 14.8 in for the french-door oven. Stated capacity: 6 qt against 26 qt.

CHEFMAN 6 Qt. Dual Basket or Emeril Lagasse 26 QT French Door

Both depth figures and both capacity figures come from the maker's own product pages, listed in the row's sources.

The trade-off: The larger oven buys the rack space and the whole-meat capacity. The smaller fryer keeps the counter usable and leaves the drawer openable. Neither figure is a price, and neither is a recommendation.

Sources: S11 · S13
