Air fryer, toaster oven or microwave: which earns the space?

You rarely need all three. Start from the job you hate doing by hand, then let the shape follow.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Most kitchens do not need three countertop appliances doing similar work. They need one, chosen well. The way to choose is not to start from the appliances; start from the task you currently do badly, slowly, or not at all.

Start with the job, not the appliance

The jobThe tool that fits it

What each shape actually costs you

A basket fryer is a small chamber with a drawer. It is quick to preheat and easy to empty, because you pull the drawer out and tip the food onto a plate. Its limit is volume: one drawer, one layer of food at a time, and a tall load cooks unevenly, which is why the baskets on the units we track come with shake reminders or viewing windows.

A countertop oven is a bigger cavity with a door, racks and usually more functions, because it is standing in for a real oven. It can run two levels at once and take food a drawer would refuse. It costs you heat and smell in the room, more pieces to wash, and real counter depth: the 26 qt unit on the maker's page measures 16.9 in deep by 15.8 in wide by 14.8 in tall (S13).

The honest middle case

The real question for most households is whether the second appliance earns its footprint. A basket air fryer and a microwave together handle the majority of weeknight cooking. A countertop oven earns its space when you genuinely cook for a group, want a second level, or want the oven's heat instead of the fryer's.

Pick one and give the rest away

Counter space is the scarcest resource in most kitchens, and the most common regret is an appliance that gets used twice a year. Work out which single job you want to hand to a machine, buy the machine that does that job well, and let the other jobs go to what you already own.

Questions readers ask next

Is an air fryer just a small convection oven?

They overlap, but the basket is closer to a shallow drawer and the countertop oven is closer to a real oven cavity. The practical differences are volume, heat recovery and how many levels you can cook on at once.

Do I need both an air fryer and a toaster oven?

Often not. If your main use is one or two foods a week for two people, a single mid-size basket machine covers it. Buy the second one when you can name the dish that needs it.

Which one heats the kitchen less?

The smaller one, generally. A basket fryer has less air to heat and a shorter preheat. A countertop oven releases more heat and more smell, which matters in a small room.
