A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quarts are the number everyone quotes and the number that misleads the most.

By Affil3 editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Air fryer capacity is usually given in quarts. That number hides three different things: how much food actually fits in one layer, whether the space is one chamber or several, and whether the quoted figure is usable volume or total volume.

A five-point checklist for any listing

  1. Is the capacity one chamber or the sum of several? Two 3-quart baskets are 6 quarts, but you cannot put a 6-quart item in them.
  2. Does the maker give litres as well as quarts? Both of the tracked Chefman pages do, which lets you check the conversion instead of trusting it.
  3. What are the basket's inside dimensions? Quarts say nothing about whether a whole chicken or a pizza fits.
  4. How full can it be loaded? Air frying needs a single layer with room for air to move; the usable capacity is smaller than the marketed one.
  5. Is the outer footprint the same as the cooking area? The 8-quart Chefman is 15.4 in deep; the 6-quart dual basket is 13.38 in deep. Measure the counter, not the capacity.

The gap between 6 and 26 quarts is not a gap in quality. It is the difference between cooking two portions at once and cooking for four to six people, which is the range the Emeril page states for its oven.

What to do with the number

Convert it into a meal. If you usually cook for two, ask whether two portions fit in one layer. If you cook for four, ask whether the basket is big enough for all four portions at once or whether you will be cooking in batches. That question is more useful than the quart figure on its own.
