A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Capacity is the first number on the box and the easiest to misread. Five checks, in order.

By Affil3 editorial team· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 6 sections

A 6-quart air fryer and a 6-quart air fryer are not the same amount of usable space. One may spread that volume across two drawers; another may pour it into one deep bucket whose shape wastes the corners. Quarts are a volume, and volume is not a cooking surface.

1. Find out whether the number is total or per drawer

The CHEFMAN 6 Qt is 6 quarts total, delivered as two independent 3-quart baskets (S11). If you compare its '6' directly against a single-basket 8-quart unit, you are comparing one number that can only be used half at a time against one that cannot. Write down both figures before you compare anything.

2. Ask how much of the volume a single layer of food actually fills

Air frying depends on air moving around the food. A full basket that is packed to the rim browns unevenly, which is why most manuals tell you to work in batches. In practice the usable capacity of any of these units is the amount that fits in one layer with a little room to spare, and that is always less than the number printed on the box.

No manufacturer page reviewed for this site states a recommended maximum load in quarts or pounds, so there is no official number to quote here. What you can do is measure the flat area of the basket or rack, which is the number that decides whether your food fits.

3. Compare the shape, not just the size

Eight quarts in one basket (S12) is a different cooking experience from six quarts in two. The single basket takes a whole chicken or a big pile of fries; the dual basket takes two smaller piles that finish at different times. Neither is more capacity in any useful sense. They are two shapes of the same question.

4. Look at the footprint per quart

Divide the counter area by the quarts and the oven-style units stop looking generous. The Emeril Lagasse 26 Qt is 26 quarts but occupies 16.9 by 15.8 inches of counter, roughly 267 square inches (S13). The CHEFMAN 6 Qt occupies 13.38 by 11.81, roughly 158 square inches (S11). You are paying for the big one in kitchen real estate as well as money.

That is not an argument against the large unit. If you cook for six people, 26 quarts is the only number here that gets dinner on the table in one pass. It is an argument for measuring first.

5. Write down what the page did not say

The Chefman 8 Qt manufacturer page does not state overall dimensions, so this site shows none for that product. That is not an oversight to be filled in later from a marketplace listing; it is the honest state of the evidence. When a page is silent, the useful action is to note the gap and check the manual when the unit arrives.

The checklist, on one screen

  1. Total quarts or per-basket quarts? Write both.
  2. What fits in one layer with room for air to move?
  3. Is the shape a single pile or two separate piles?
  4. Square inches of counter per quart of capacity.
  5. Which number on the page was missing, and where will you get it?
No capacity on this page is described as better. The units differ in what they are for; the checklist is only about reading the numbers honestly.
