Do air fryer presets do anything you cannot do yourself?

A preset is a start point somebody else chose. Here is what the official pages actually say about the counts they advertise.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

A preset count is the easiest number to put in a product title and the hardest to confirm on a specification block. Three rows in this small shelf answer it three different ways, and reading them together is more useful than any single one.

Three machines, three kinds of answer

ApplianceWhat the listing statesWhat the maker states
Emeril Lagasse 26 QT French Door24 one-touch preset options, in the listing titleIts own page disagrees with itself: 25 cooking pre-sets in the specification strip, 24 one-touch cooking presets in the description. No count is published here.
Chefman TurboFry Touch 8 Qt (RJ38-SQSS-8T)Named functions such as bake, dehydrate and frozen in the listing title4 cooking presets, stated on the maker's product page
CHEFMAN 6 Qt Dual Basket (RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W)Digital touch controls in the listing titleFunctions rather than a count: Sync Finish, Hi-Fry, a plus-two-minute control and a shake reminder

Put those three rows in a row and a pattern appears. A preset count describes buttons, not cooking ability. One maker publishes functions instead of a number, one publishes a number its own page contradicts, and one publishes a small count against a temperature range that lets you do the same job by hand.

What a preset is actually worth

  1. Check the temperature range first. Chefman states 200-400 °F for the 8 Qt and a 450 °F ceiling for the dual-basket model's Hi-Fry function. If the settings your recipes ask for already sit inside that range, a button saves you two dial turns and nothing else.
  2. Then look for what you cannot set by hand. Neither page for these models states a multi-stage programme, so a recipe that says 400 °F for ten minutes then 320 °F for eight is two manual steps whatever the button count.
  3. Treat accessories as a separate question. Presets do not add a rack, a tray or a rotisserie spit. The Emeril Lagasse listing names six cooking accessories in its title while the maker's description says seven, which is its own flagged disagreement.

The one case where presets earn their place

A preset is a shortcut for a food you cook on repeat, in the same basket, at the same load. Frozen fries from a 3 qt drawer are exactly that, and a button there is a small, real saving. A dinner you are trying to time is not: the timing problem is the surface area of a basket, and no count changes it.

Where two official pages disagree about a number, this site treats it as a flag rather than a specification. Both readings stay visible on the product row and the disagreement is listed on the Flagged for review page.

Questions readers ask next

Does more presets mean better cooking?

No source cited on this site says that. The two Chefman pages state functions and a 4-preset count without describing cooking outcomes, and the Emeril Lagasse count differs between two official pages.

Should I ignore presets completely?

Ignore them as a ranking number. Read them as a list of start points, then go back to basket volume and temperature range, which is where a weeknight is decided.

Are dehydrate and bake modes worth paying for?

They are named on the 8 Qt listing and on Chefman's page for that model. Time ranges, tray counts and any accessory each mode needs are not stated on either page, so read the manual before you plan a weekend of chips.

Why does this site not just pick a number?

Because the average of two conflicting figures is a third number that no official page states. Amazon's program terms also bar using a site to present price and promotion data the program has not authorised, so a count that cannot be confirmed stays unconfirmed and the reason is published.
