Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Two shapes, two different kitchens. Decide this before you look at a single model number.

By Affil3 editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

A basket air fryer is a drawer with a heating element and a fan above it. An oven-style air fryer is a small countertop oven that uses the same fast-moving air. They are sold as if they were the same appliance in different clothes. In practice they suit different cooking.

The three questions that actually decide it

  1. What is the biggest single thing you cook? A whole chicken or a 12-inch pizza needs a door and a rack, not a drawer.
  2. Do you cook two foods on two different timings? That points at two baskets, or at an oven with two racks and a single shared temperature.
  3. How much counter and cabinet space do you have? A drawer pulls out; French doors open forward. Both need clearance you may not have.

Pick the shape, then pick the size

Once the shape is settled, the size question is usually a question about your counter. Measure the depth with the drawer open or the doors swung out, not just the closed footprint. The three tracked appliances need between 10.6 and 14.8 inches of height and between 12.6 and 16.9 inches of depth.

What this guide does not do

It does not tell you which brand is better. It has not tested any of these appliances, and the chefman and Emeril pages on this site say the same. If you want the shape decision in a single table, the comparison page has it.

One thing worth knowing before you shop: on two of the tracked listings the advertised headline disagrees with the manufacturer's own specification sheet. The Chefman 8 Qt listing advertises 450 °F where Chefman publishes 400 °F, and the Chefman 6 Qt listing calls the product an oven where its own page describes a two-drawer basket fryer. Read the maker's specification, not the listing title.
