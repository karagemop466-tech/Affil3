Dual basket or one big basket: which fits your weeknights?

Two small drawers or one large one is a counter-space question before it is a capacity question. Here is what the two maker pages state.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Two drawers sounds like more cooking than one drawer. It is not, unless the food you cook wants two timers at once. Start with what lands on your table on a Tuesday, then read the two spec sheets against each other.

Both columns come from Chefman's own product pages for those exact model strings, with the Amazon listing as the second reading. The dual-basket page labels its case dimensions depth, width and height. The 8 qt page gives three numbers with no labels, so which one is your cabinet height is your measurement to take, not theirs to read off.

The split nobody prints on the box

A 3 qt basket holds a 3 qt basket's worth. Two of them do not become one 6 qt chamber, so a batch that needs a single layer has to be divided between drawers. Where the split pays is two foods that want different clocks and one finishing moment: the dual-window model carries Sync Finish and a window on each drawer, both stated on the maker's page.

The single 8 qt chamber is the opposite trade. One food, one temperature, more surface, and no divider deciding how much of the basket your chicken thighs are allowed to claim.

Counter maths, in inches

  • The 8 qt unit is the taller of the two on paper, 13.5 in against 10.63 in, so a cabinet 15 in above the counter is the number to check before you order it.
  • Neither machine is light enough to live in a cupboard nightly: 13.3 lb and 12.8 lb, which is a two-hands job with a full basket.
  • Neither page states a required clearance gap around the unit, so the air space behind it and above it is yours to decide and yours to measure.

Which one to pick

Two drawers if a weeknight for you is a protein and a vegetable on different timers, and each of them fits one layer inside 3 qt. One 8 qt basket if the meal is a single big batch and you have the vertical room. Both of those statements are about published volume and published case size. Nobody here has cooked in either machine, and neither maker publishes a servings figure for these two models, so this page stops where the documents stop.

Every number above is from a manufacturer product page or the observed Amazon listing, both linked from the shop row for that model. None of it is a measurement this site took.

Questions readers ask next

Is a dual-basket machine really two air fryers?

It is one machine with two baskets. Chefman's page for RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W states a single 1700 W / 120 V figure alongside the two 3 qt baskets, so the drawers share one published power specification.

Is 6 qt of two baskets the same as 6 qt of one?

No, because shape matters more than the sum. Two shallow drawers hold two single-layer foods; one 6 qt chamber holds one food with more depth. That is the whole argument on both sides, and it is why the case-size row above matters more than the total quarts.

Do either of them have a viewing window?

The dual-window model is named for it: the maker's page and the listing title both state a window per basket. For the 8 Qt single basket, neither the maker's page nor the listing states a window, so this site does not claim one.

Which is easier to clean?

Both pages say the baskets go in a dishwasher; Chefman adds top-rack for the 8 Qt basket and rack. Coating life, rack count and whether the drawer body can get wet are not stated by either page.
