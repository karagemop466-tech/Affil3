How much counter space does an air fryer really need?

Measure the space before you shop, and add the room the drawer takes when it is open.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026 · 577 words, about a 3 minute read

The short answer

The number that decides the purchase is not the capacity on the box but the outside footprint plus the clearance the maker asks for. Measure the space, add the room a drawer takes when it is open or a door takes when it swings, and check it against the footprints published for the three countertop appliances on this shelf.

Counter space is the constraint that quietly decides most kitchen purchases. You can talk about wattage and presets for an hour, and the appliance still has to live on a strip of worktop between the sink and the hob. So start with a tape measure, not a product page.

Three dimensions are not enough

A manufacturer lists a size, usually depth by width by height. That is the size of the box. What you need is the size of the box plus the space it takes up while you use it, and those are different numbers.

Measure these four things before you shop

What to measureWhy it matters
Depth of the run you haveThe manufacturer's stated depth, plus a little slack for the plug and the cable.
Width of the run you haveThe stated width, plus enough to lift a basket out or swing a door open.
Height between the counter and the cabinet aboveThe stated height, plus the clearance you need to lift the basket up and out.
The clear floor space in frontBasket drawers pull out. A fryer with a full-width drawer needs that room every time you cook, not only on the day it arrives.

The drawer is the part that catches people

A basket fryer is a drawer you pull out to shake the food. The 6 qt dual-basket unit we track is 10.63 in tall, which sounds modest until you try to lift two 3 qt baskets out of it under a wall cabinet. Measure the height of the space above the counter as well as the space in front of the unit. This is the single most common way a kitchen-appliance purchase goes wrong.

Doors swing, drawers pull

The french-door oven is a different problem: 14.8 in tall and 16.9 in deep, with doors that open outward. It is the largest thing on this list and the one that most needs a clear run of worktop in front of it. If your counter is an L-shape with the hob at the end, an appliance that needs 16 in of depth in front of it may not be the one that fits.

A rule of thumb you can actually use

If you want a straight answer: take the largest dimension of the unit you are considering and double it in your head. A 15 in appliance wants roughly 15 in of counter to sit on and roughly 15 in of room to work in. That rule is not from a manufacturer; it is a sanity check, and it is wrong in one direction only, always toward buying something smaller than you wanted.

Questions readers ask next

Does an air fryer need clearance above it?

Yes, more than a toaster does. You lift the basket out to shake it or check the food, and you need room to do that without hitting a cabinet.

Is the stated size the size of the packaging?

No. The sizes published on the product pages we cite are the unit's own dimensions. Boxes are larger, but a box only matters on the day it arrives.

What if I want the largest one and it does not fit?

Decide which of the two you want more: the capacity or the footprint. We would rather you buy the smaller one and use it than buy the bigger one and shelve it.
