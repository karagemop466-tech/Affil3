Dual basket or single basket: which air fryer do you need?

Two drawers that finish together is a different promise from one deep basket. Here is what each shape actually commits you to.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Almost every air fryer comparison starts with capacity, and capacity is exactly where the two shapes stop being comparable. A six-quart machine split into two three-quart baskets does not hold six quarts of anything at once. It holds three quarts in one drawer and three in the other, and the two finish on their own clocks. A single eight-quart basket holds eight quarts in one chamber. If your dinner is one big batch, those are different appliances. If your dinner is two dishes with different cooking times, they are different again.

We track two Chefman TurboFry Touch air fryers that make this comparison concrete, and we read both the manufacturer's specification pages and the Amazon listings the short links land on. Where those two disagree, we say so instead of picking a side.

Both figures come from Chefman's own product pages for these model numbers. Two cells are blank on purpose: the manufacturer does not publish a preset count for the two-basket unit, and neither page mentions a viewing window for the single-basket unit. We do not fill those in from the listing titles.

The 8 qt listing on Amazon carries a title that advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry burst, and its own bullet list states 200-400 °F. The manufacturer publishes 200-400 °F for model RJ38-SQSS-8T. We publish the manufacturer's figure and flag the title claim rather than repeating it as a specification.

Read the temperature ceiling as a promise about your food, not a race

The 450 °F on the two-basket unit is a real published figure with a named setting attached: Hi-Fry. The 400 °F ceiling on the single-basket unit is simply the top of its range. That difference matters less than it looks. Air frying at 400 °F crisps thin, breaded food perfectly well; 450 °F is where you go when you want the very darkest edges on something dense. If your weeknight food is chicken wings and fries, the gap between the two ceilings is unlikely to be the reason you pick one over the other.

Wattage, and the circuit it lives on

Both units publish 1700 W at 120 V. That is worth knowing for a reason that has nothing to do with cooking speed: 1700 W at 120 V draws roughly 14 amps, which is close to what a 15-amp kitchen circuit is rated for, and the same circuit usually carries the toaster. Plug either unit in alone and you are fine. Plug one in while the toaster is running and you may be looking for the reset button.

The 8 qt listing states 1000 W for the same model number the manufacturer names, RJ38-SQSS-8T. We publish 1700 W because that is what Chefman states on the product page, and we flag the difference rather than averaging it or quietly dropping it.

Counter space, with the drawer open

The two-basket unit is the shorter and deeper of the two at 13.38 x 11.81 x 10.63 in, and it is 13.3 lb. The single-basket unit is taller and wider at 15.4 x 12.6 x 13.5 in, and 12.8 lb. Neither is small. But the number that decides whether it fits is not the footprint in the manual. It is the footprint with the drawer pulled out, because you pull it out every time and you need somewhere to put the hot basket while you do.

Two drawers open side by side means you need clear space across a wider stretch of counter at once. One deep drawer means you need clear space in front of a single unit. Neither figure is published, so measure your own counter rather than trusting the case size.

Cleanup, in one line

Both baskets are nonstick and both are dishwasher-safe, so neither wins on the washing-up question. The single-basket unit's page adds that the rack is top-rack dishwasher safe. The two-basket unit has two baskets to wash instead of one, which is the part the dishwasher rating does not tell you.

So which one do you need?

  1. If you cook one big batch of one thing, you want the single deep basket. Splitting a batch across two baskets means the food in the emptier basket finishes sooner and dries out.
  2. If you cook two things that need different times, you want the two-basket unit. Separate drawers mean separate timers, and the Sync Finish function is designed to hold both to the same end time.
  3. If you are undecided, look at your counter and your outlet before the features. A unit you cannot plug in safely is the wrong unit.
  4. If you want to see the numbers side by side with the rest of the range, the shop table and the comparison page have every published figure for both of these and the other three we track.

Questions readers ask next

Is a two-basket air fryer quieter than a single-basket one?

Neither manufacturer publishes a sound level for either model, so we have no figure to compare and we will not estimate one. If noise is the deciding factor for you, that is a question for the retailer or a review you trust, not for a specification sheet.

Does the Sync Finish function mean both baskets cook at the same temperature?

The manufacturer describes Sync Finish as a function so both baskets finish cooking at the same time, with a Sync function so both baskets finish cooking simultaneously. It is described as a timing feature. The page does not describe it as forcing two different temperature settings to converge, so we do not describe it that way either.

Can I use parchment in either basket?

Neither manufacturer page states a parchment rule, so there is no published answer on this site. Check the manual or the manufacturer's own support page for your model before using it.

Why is there no preset count for the two-basket unit?

Because the manufacturer's specification page for model RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W does not publish one. The Amazon listing for that unit does not state a preset count either. A blank cell on our table means we could not confirm the figure from an official page, not that the feature is missing.
