How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

You will clean this thing more often than you will admire it, and you will look at it every day. Both costs are knowable before you buy.

By Affil3 editorial team· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 5 sections

Two costs never make it onto the marketing photo: how much of your evening the cleanup takes, and how much of your counter the appliance permanently owns. Both can be judged from a manufacturer page if you know which sentences to look for.

Cleanup: read the dishwasher sentence carefully

There is a difference between 'dishwasher safe' and 'top rack dishwasher safe', and a further difference between the basket and the rest of the unit. The Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt page says the nonstick basket and rack are top-rack dishwasher safe (S12). The CHEFMAN 6 Qt page says its baskets are nonstick and dishwasher safe (S11). Neither page claims the housing can go in the sink, and no page reviewed here claims the heating element can be washed at all.

The second cost: the counter, measured two ways

Counter space has a footprint and a clearance. Footprint is the base of the unit; clearance is the room it needs to work. A basket fryer needs room in front of it for the drawer to slide out. An oven-style unit needs room for a door that opens toward you, and the Emeril's dimensions of 16.9 inches deep by 14.8 tall (S13) mean the door swing is not trivial.

Go stand at your counter with a tape measure. Mark the footprint with painter's tape, then open a cupboard door in the same space to represent the drawer or the oven door. This takes two minutes and settles arguments that product photos never will.

Windows and lights change how often you clean

A window with an interior light (S11) means you check on food without pulling the drawer. Every pull drops heat, and every drop in heat is a minute added at the end. The single-basket TurboFry 8 Qt does not list a window, so checking means opening it (S12). Over a year of cooking, that is a lot of extra openings, and a lot of extra time standing over the unit.

What nobody tells you about the corners

The awkward part of cleaning any air fryer is the corner where the basket meets the crisper plate, and the underside of the plate itself. A removable plate that lifts out is easier than one that is fixed into the drawer. None of the manufacturer pages reviewed here describes whether the crisper plate is removable, so that is a question for the manual or the box before you commit.

A practical order of operations

  1. Find the dishwasher sentence on the manufacturer page and note exactly which parts it covers.
  2. Measure the footprint on your counter with tape.
  3. Test the drawer or door clearance with a cupboard door.
  4. Decide whether a window is worth it to you. Then decide the size.
