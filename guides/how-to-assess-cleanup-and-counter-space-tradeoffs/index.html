How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The appliance you use is the one that is easy to clean and has somewhere to live.

By Affil3 editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Two things get decided after the purchase and are rarely discussed before it: where the appliance lives, and how much of it has to be washed. Both are answerable from the manufacturer's published dimensions and materials list.

Measure three things, not one

  1. The closed footprint. Every maker publishes this: the tracked appliances range from 13.38 in to 16.9 in deep.
  2. The space it needs to open. A drawer pulls straight out; French doors swing forward. Measure with the appliance open, and check what is above it if the lid or door lifts.
  3. The clearance to the wall and to the cabinet above, which decides whether the heat and steam have anywhere to go. The manufacturer's manual states this; the product page often does not.

A dual-basket appliance has two of everything, which is twice the washing but also means one basket can soak while the other cooks. An oven has racks and a tray and a door with a gasket and crevices.

The counter-space test, in one minute

Clear the counter space you think you have. Put a sheet of paper down at the footprint of the appliance you are considering, then put a second sheet where the drawer or doors would open. If both fit without moving anything you use daily, the appliance has a home. If you have to move the kettle every time, it does not, and it will end up in a cupboard.

Cleaning questions to ask before you buy

  • Which parts are nonstick, and does the maker say they are dishwasher-safe? Nonstick coatings survive hand-washing longer.
  • Is there a window, and does it need cleaning inside and out? A window saves opening the drawer but adds a surface.
  • Where do the crumbs and grease go? A basket with a removable crisper plate is easier to wash than one with a fixed base.
  • Does the maker publish a cleaning section in the manual? If it does, read it before you buy; it is the most honest description of what the appliance is like to own.
