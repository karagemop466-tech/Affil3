How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The best-looking appliance is the one you will actually clean, and the one that still fits when the drawer is open.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Appliance regret almost never comes from cooking performance. It comes from the two taxes you pay every single day: the washing, and the space. Both are predictable before you buy, if you ask the right questions.

The cleanup questions, in order

1. Count the pieces that touch food

A basket fryer is one basket and maybe a crisper plate. An oven-style unit can be two racks, a fry basket, a crumb tray, and a rotisserie spit. Every piece that touched grease needs washing after every greasy cook. Before you buy, list the removable parts from the listing photos and imagine the drying rack after taco night.

2. Read dishwasher claims precisely

Titles are careful with this, and so should you be. On our shelf, one observed listing titles its two baskets as dishwasher-safe, and another titles a single nonstick basket the same way. Neither phrase tells you whether racks, trays, or the housing wipe down easily; it only covers named pieces. Nonstick also has a service life: metal utensils and abrasive sponges shorten it, so the claim survives only as long as your habits are gentle.

3. Judge the grease path

Grease lands where the air ends. In basket units it pools under a removable basket, which is convenient. In oven styles it can drip toward the door and the crumb tray, which is a wider wipe. Door glass, interior walls, and heating-element guards all earn a spot on the cleaning schedule eventually. Look at interior photos for how far food spatter can travel.

The counter-space questions

MeasureHow to take itWhat people forget
FootprintWidth and depth of the base, plus vibration room at the backThe back needs ventilation clearance, not just the sides
Open clearanceDepth of the drawer or door swing in useYou open it while cooking, so the space must be free at dinner time, not just in the store photo
HeightMachine height plus anything you place on top nowCabinet overhangs and shelves above eat usable height
WeightCheck the listing's shipping weight as a proxyHeavy units do not move for cleaning behind and under, and that chore matters

One trick that saves returns: outline the machine's footprint in painter's tape on the counter, then outline the open drawer in front of it. Live with the tape for two days. Most people discover the open-drawer outline crosses either the sink or the stove, and that discovery is cheaper in tape than in shipping.

The trade-off, stated plainly

Big capacity buys you fewer cooking batches and costs you more washing and more counter. Small capacity keeps cleanup to one basket and gives the counter back, at the price of cooking in rounds. Neither is wrong; the mistake is paying for capacity you use twice a year while washing the price of it every night.

Questions readers ask next

Do I need to clean the heating element?

Follow the manual's guidance for the specific unit. What we can say generally: grease reaches the area above the food chamber over time, and units designed with easier access to that area make the deep clean less of a project.

Is dishwasher-safe the same as durable?

No. Dishwasher-safe describes surviving the wash; nonstick coatings still age with abrasive contact. Gentle washing extends the life of any coated piece.

Can I store the appliance in a cabinet?

Yes, if you will actually lift it in and out. Weight is the deciding factor, which is why we suggest checking listing weight before choosing an under-cabinet life.
