Is a Black Friday air fryer deal worth waiting for?

This site publishes no prices, so this is not a deal page. It is the checklist a discounted appliance still has to pass.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Any guide in this category can tell you what to compare. None of them can tell you whether a discount is real, and this page is no different. What it can do is hand you the four checks a sale-priced appliance still has to pass, in the order they catch a bad buy.

Check one: the model string, not the name

The words 8 qt air fryer can describe two different machines. Chefman sells RJ38-SQSS-8T, whose own page states 8 qt / 7.57 L and a 200-400 °F range, and Chefman sells RJ38-8T-BLACK, a different 8 Qt unit with a 450 °F Hi-Fry mode and a matte black finish. This project made that mistake itself: an earlier revision of one row was compared against the wrong Chefman page and had to be corrected, and the wrong comparison is still visible in the register so nobody repeats it. A discounted listing that omits the model string is a listing you cannot verify, whatever it costs.

Check two: the fields a sale page blurs

FieldWhere the official answer livesWhat a deal page leaves out
Basket volumeThe maker's product page for that model stringThe difference between two small baskets and one large chamber
Case sizeThe maker's specification blockWhether the three numbers are labelled depth, width and height
Temperature ceilingThe maker's page, plus the listing where the two disagreeWhich of the two the box inside matches
WarrantyThe maker's warranty lineWhether activation is required, and by when

Check three: what this kind of guide may not show you

A site in the Amazon Associates program may display price and availability only where Amazon serves that data or it arrives through the Creators or Product Advertising API. This site has no API access and no price feed, so it shows no prices, no availability and no star ratings, in deal season or any other week. That is why nothing here goes stale overnight, and why the listing is still the only place a number is live.

Check four: whether it is the thing you needed

  • Replacing a basket whose coating lifted: the warranty line and the dishwasher statement decide it, not the discount.
  • First appliance: the question is capacity and counter height, and the answer starts with how many people you feed at once rather than with a percentage.
  • Waiting for one specific model: the useful work is knowing its model string and its published numbers, which is the part a sale page is worst at stating clearly.

The takedown promise on this page

Seasonal pages here carry an obligation instead of a celebration. Amazon's participation requirements ask that links and references to a limited-time promotion be removed as soon as that promotion ends, so a guide that leans on a sale event is written to be taken down after it. That decision is recorded on the site's own question board rather than left to someone's memory, and the same rule is why this site names no event dates and no discounts.

Questions readers ask next

Do you know when the sale starts?

No. Amazon sets its own event timing, and this site republishes no promotional dates and no prices. The date in the byline above is the date the specifications were last checked against the official pages.

Is an older model on offer a bad buy?

Not automatically. Older and newer model strings can differ in the two places a spec sheet is read for, basket volume and temperature ceiling, so compare the string against the maker's page before deciding. One row on this site also records a listing that Amazon itself presents alongside a newer variant.

Will you add price tracking later?

Only with Amazon's authorisation. Price tracking and price alerting on a site in this program need approval, which is a decision on the board, not a feature someone can quietly switch on.

How do I know your own links are not steering me to a sale?

Because no sale is linked anywhere on this site. Every outbound product link goes to an Amazon detail page, carries the disclosure and is marked sponsored, which is the same answer we give on a Tuesday in March.
