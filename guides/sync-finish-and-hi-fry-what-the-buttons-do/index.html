Sync Finish and Hi-Fry: what the buttons do

Dual-basket machines sell themselves on three named features. Two are about timing, one is about temperature, and none of them is about how well food cooks.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026 · 687 words, about a 3 minute read

The short answer

Sync Finish is a timing feature - the machine starts one basket later so two foods land together - and it does not equalise temperature or method between the sides. Independent baskets buy two temperatures and two timers, but each chamber stays 3 quarts. And the 450 °F Hi-Fry ceiling is stated for the dual-basket model only: the same brand's 8 qt page states 200-400 °F, so the higher claim is named and flagged rather than adopted.

Feature names on an appliance panel are marketing sentences compressed into two words. Unpack them and you get a much better idea of whether a machine suits how you actually cook. Three names carry almost all the weight on the dual-basket unit we track, and each one means less than the button suggests.

The Chefman page for this model describes a 6-quart air fryer that “combines two powerful air fryers in one, allowing you to cook different dishes simultaneously or easily switch between baskets”. The Amazon listing title is more specific: two independent 3-quart baskets. Whether that shape suits your week at all is a separate question, answered on its own page; this one is about what the named buttons actually do.

Sync Finish is a timing feature, not a cooking feature

The manufacturer describes a Sync Finish feature “to ensure both baskets finish cooking simultaneously”. That is the whole promise: the machine manages when one side starts so two foods land on the table together. It does not equalise temperature between the sides, it does not make a chicken thigh and a tray of fries cook alike, and it will not rescue a dish that needed a different technique. If your Tuesday problem is two things, one plug, same table, it is aimed squarely at that. If your problem is even browning across one large batch, it is not.

Hi-Fry is a number, and the number is disputed

On the dual-basket page the manufacturer states a maximum of 450 °F (235 °C) with the Hi-Fry function, and the listing title lists Hi-Fry among the features. On the 8 qt single-basket Chefman the two documents part company: the maker's page for the model the listing names (RJ38-SQSS-8T) states 200-400 °F, while the Amazon title advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry option - and 450 °F is what the maker states for a different 8 qt unit, the TurboFry Touch with High Fry. A headline can be a promise about a product family; a specification block is a claim about one unit. This site publishes the 400 °F figure for that model and leaves the title's 450 °F claim named but unadopted (FLG-027). When two official pages conflict, the conservative figure goes on the page and the conflict goes in the register.

Three questions to ask of any dual-basket listing

  1. What is the capacity of one basket, not of the pair? Compare a single batch against a single chamber, not against a total.
  2. Which page states the temperature ceiling - the maker's specification or the listing title? If they differ, the difference belongs in your decision, not in a footnote.
  3. Is there a care statement about the baskets - nonstick, dishwasher-safe, top rack only? Washing is part of the daily cost of owning this shape, and it is a separate claim from the cooking one.

No appliance was operated to write this guide. Every feature sentence above is a statement from a manufacturer page or from the observed Amazon listing, and the two are attributed separately because they are not always the same sentence.

Questions readers ask next

Do I need Sync Finish if I can set a timer myself?

You can do the same arithmetic by hand. The feature saves you starting one side late and watching two countdowns, which on a busy evening is most of its value.

Is Hi-Fry worth paying for?

That cannot be answered from these pages, because they state what the function is - a higher temperature ceiling - and not how it performs. We do not publish a performance opinion we cannot source.

Can a dual-basket unit replace a single big drawer for a family?

Only if your batches fit in 3 quarts each. For one large single item, the 8 qt single-basket shape exists for exactly that reason, which is why both shapes stay on our shelf.
