Cooking thermometers · Guide

Is your meat thermometer telling you the right number?

A thermometer is only as useful as the number you compare it with.

By Affil3 editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Working title. The temperatures below are the USDA's published minimums. This guide does not test the accuracy of any thermometer.

The minimum internal temperatures

  • Beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts: 145 °F, then rest for at least 3 minutes.
  • Ground meats: 160 °F.
  • Ground poultry: 165 °F.
  • All poultry, including breasts, whole birds, legs, thighs and wings: 165 °F.
  • Fresh or smoked ham that is uncooked: 145 °F, then rest for at least 3 minutes.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart.

Where to put the probe

For a whole bird, the USDA says to check the innermost part of the thigh, the innermost part of the wing and the thickest part of the breast, avoiding bone. For poultry parts, insert the probe into the thickest area, avoiding bone.

Questions to ask about a wireless thermometer

  • What temperature range and probe count does the manufacturer state?
  • Does the manufacturer describe how accuracy was tested? If it does not, write that down.
  • Does the app need an account, and what does the privacy policy say it collects?
  • Is the probe rated for the temperature you will cook at? Check the manufacturer's stated limit.
