Thermometers | Buying guide

Is your meat thermometer telling you the right number?

A thermometer is only useful if the number is right, in the right place, compared against the right target.

By Affil3 editorial team· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 5 sections

Most complaints about meat thermometers are really complaints about one of three things: the probe was in the wrong spot, the reading was taken before the temperature had settled, or the cook had no reference number to compare against. All three are fixable before you spend anything.

Start with the number you are aiming for

The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service publishes safe minimum internal temperatures, measured with a food thermometer before the food leaves the heat source. The ones that matter most for home cooking are below, quoted from the FSIS chart (S10).

Safe minimum internal temperatures, quoted from the FSIS chart. Rest times are part of the instruction, not a suggestion.
FoodMinimum internal temperatureRest
Beef, pork, veal, lamb steaks, chops, roasts145 °F (62.8 °C)3 minutes
Ground meats160 °F (71.1 °C)none stated
All poultry, including ground poultry165 °F (73.9 °C)none stated
Fish and shellfish145 °F (62.8 °C)none stated
Leftovers and casseroles165 °F (73.9 °C)none stated
Eggs160 °F (71.1 °C)none stated
FSIS notes that these are minimums and that personal preference may lead you to cook higher. It also says to measure with a food thermometer, not by colour or by time.

Where to put the probe

For a whole bird, the thickest part of the thigh is a reliable spot, along with the thickest part of the breast, staying away from bone and from the cavity. Bone conducts heat differently and will give you a reading that is not the meat. For a roast, go into the centre of the thickest part, not the end.

A leave-in probe stays in the meat and reports continuously. The CHEF iQ Sense uses four internal sensors and one ambient sensor, with an ambient range up to 1000 °F, and relays readings through a Wi-Fi hub (S14). That ambient sensor is the part people skip: it tells you the temperature around the food, which is what actually drives cooking time and what catches a flare-up before it ruins the cook.

An instant-read probe is a spot check. You open the lid, stab, read, and close. It is faster to set up, it needs no app, and it cannot tell you anything while the lid is closed. Neither kind is better; they answer different questions.

The claims worth being sceptical about

Accuracy claims like plus or minus half a degree look impressive next to a safe-minimum table measured in whole degrees. They matter far less than probe placement and settling time. A perfectly accurate probe in the wrong spot is worse than an average probe in the right one, because it gives you a precise number you should not trust.

Two specific conflicts are recorded on the Flagged for review page: the CHEF iQ manufacturer's own pages disagree on battery life (70+ hours on one page, up to 60 on the FAQ), and the ThermoMaven unit has no manufacturer page at all, so none of its specifications are published here.

A 60-second calibration check

  1. Fill a glass with crushed ice and water, stir, and wait a minute.
  2. Insert the probe without touching the glass.
  3. It should read close to 32 °F. If it is far off, the probe is the problem, not your cooking.
  4. Repeat at the start of the season, not the middle of a dinner party.
