24 presets or 25? How to read a preset list

The preset count is the one number on the box that cannot be compared between brands. Here is what to check instead, worked through one machine we actually track.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026 · 559 words, about a 2 minute read

The short answer

The Emeril 26 QT oven states its own preset count two ways: the manufacturer page's specification strip reads 25 cooking pre-sets while that same page's description, and the Amazon listing title, read 24 one-touch presets; the function count appears as 10-in-1 in prose and 11-in-1 in a page image. This site publishes the disagreement rather than a number. The comparable fields are capacity, the maker's own serving line, fan behaviour, outside dimensions, voltage and the accessories actually named.

A preset count looks like a spec and behaves like a slogan. The number is real - somebody counted buttons - but the unit is arbitrary, because nothing standardises what a preset is. It is the one figure on a box on which two brands cannot be compared, and it is usually the first number a listing leads with.

A worked example, from a machine we actually track

The Emeril Lagasse 26 QT French Door oven states its own preset count two different ways on the same manufacturer page. Its specification strip reads “25 Cooking Pre-sets”. The description on that same page opens “This endlessly versatile 10-in-1 air fry convection oven…”. The Amazon listing title reads “24 One-Touch Preset Options”. And the manufacturer page carries an image whose file name and on-page note record an eleven-function count. Four figures from two official pages about one machine, each of them published somewhere the brand controls.

So what went on the product page? None of them, as a fact. The disagreement is recorded, sourced and dated (FLG-024), and the fields a shopper can act on are the ones both pages agree on. That is this site's whole editorial method in miniature: no reconciliation, no averaging, no quietly picking the friendlier number.

If you still want to judge the presets

Then judge them on the three things that make a preset usable, none of which a count expresses: what temperature and time each preset applies; whether the presets cover the foods you actually cook (wings and roasted vegetables are common, a genuine dehydrate step and a bake step are not universal); and whether you can leave a preset and set time and temperature by hand in two presses. That last one decides whether the panel helps you or gets in your way once you stop cooking from buttons.

No timing, browning or doneness claim appears on this page, because no source we hold supports one. This is a guide to reading a specification sheet, not a review of the appliance.

Questions readers ask next

Is 25 presets better than 24?

Neither. There is no common definition of a preset, and here the same manufacturer's page gives both answers depending on which part of it you read. A count can only tell you roughly how much of the panel is shortcuts.

Should I trust the listing title or the manufacturer page?

Trust whichever one states the claim you can corroborate elsewhere on the same page, and treat the disagreement as information. Where a claim sits on one page only, this site publishes it as that page's claim - “the maker states…” - and not as a fact about the product.

What about 10-in-1 versus 11-in-1?

The same problem one level up: it depends what a company counts as a function. If a specific mode matters to you - dehydrate, proof, rotisserie - look for that mode named with a temperature range, not for a total.

Every specification on our product pages is shown with the page that states it, and every disagreement between two official pages has its own row in the flag register.
