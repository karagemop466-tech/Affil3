Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

A twenty-four preset machine is a promise. These are the questions that find out whether the machine does what it says, before the box is open.

Autumn 2026 7 min read Published 2026-11-20 Desk: The Counter & Crumb desk
The short answer

Find out how many of those functions you will really use, then make sure the one you will use most is the best-executed one rather than the most advertised. Presets are easy to add to a product page and hard to make good. Then check the three things every multi-function appliance gets wrong somewhere: the size of the cavity, the number of pieces you have to clean, and what happens when you want to cook two things at once.

The question

What should I check before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance with a lot of presets?

Presets are a marketing surface, not a feature list

The Emeril Lagasse in this register is listed with 24 one-touch preset options and six cooking accessories included. Both facts come straight from the official listing title, and both are the kind of thing that reads as generosity in a search result.

The honest test is narrower: how many of those 24 presets correspond to a dish you will actually cook, and how many times a month? Most households use two or three. If the appliance has 24 ways to do it and one excellent way, you are paying for the other 23.

So the first question is not 'how many presets', it is 'which preset'. Find out which cooking mode the machine is genuinely best at, and whether that mode is the one you would use most.

The cavity question

A multi-function appliance is a small oven. Small ovens have a known geometry problem: the heating elements are close to the food, so the top of a tall item browns before the middle cooks. That is the failure mode to look for.

What you want to know: how tall is the clear internal space, where are the elements relative to the rack, and does the machine have a convection fan for when you are baking rather than frying.

We could not confirm the internal geometry of any of the appliances in this register from an official source, so we have published none of it. These are the questions to put to the manufacturer.

The two-at-once question

Multi-function appliances advertise capacity as a single number, which hides the useful distinction: can the machine cook two things at once, and if so, at the same temperature?

The three air fryers in this register answer this in three different ways, and the official titles make each answer readable. The dual basket has two independent 3 quart baskets. The French door oven has one extra large interior. The 8 quart TurboFry has one XL basket.

Read the word 'independent' in a listing title. It is doing more work than it looks like.

The cleaning question again, but worse

A four-function machine has four kinds of mess. A twenty-four-preset oven has racks, a drip tray, a rotisserie spit, a basket and a door, and it has to handle all of them.

Here is the test we would apply. Count the removable parts. Then ask the manufacturer which of them are dishwasher-safe, because a rotisserie spit is never dishwasher-safe and you will find out at the sink.

Six questions to put to a manufacturer

Before you buy, ask these and write the answers down.

  • Which preset is this machine genuinely best at?
  • What is the clear internal height and width of the cavity, in inches?
  • Where are the heating elements relative to the food, and how do you avoid the top burning before the middle cooks?
  • Can two items cook at once, and at different temperatures?
  • Which removable parts are dishwasher-safe?
  • What is the warranty on the heating elements and the electronics, and who services it?

And one question about the guarantee

The last one matters more than people expect. A multi-function appliance has more failure points than a fryer: more elements, more sensors, a control board, a door with a switch on it.

Check what the manufacturer actually warrants and what the return window is. Some of these products are sold through third-party sellers, and the warranty can be handled entirely differently depending on who shipped it. We would want that confirmed before spending real money.

Questions people ask

Are preset programs a sign of a good appliance?

No. A preset is one setting of temperature and time. Whether it produces a good result depends on the airflow and the element placement, which the listing does not tell you.

Is a 24-preset appliance better than a 4-preset one?

Only if you use the extra twenty. Our view is that the number of presets measures marketing effort more reliably than it measures capability.

Do I need the rotisserie and dehydrator modes?

Only if you will use them. The dehydrate mode on the Chefman 8 quart TurboFry listing is paired with bake and Hi-Fry, which is a more balanced set of modes for most kitchens.
