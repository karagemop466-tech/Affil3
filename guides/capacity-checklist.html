THE NO-HYPE CHECKLIST · WORKING GUIDE

A practical checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Capacity figures can be hard to picture. Use this worksheet to translate a label into the portions and cooking flow you need.

5 MIN READ · EDITORIAL CHECKLIST · NOT A PRODUCT RATING

Capacity is not just a number on a box. The useful question is whether an appliance can handle the amount you cook, in a way that fits your routine. Compare exact model details; do not assume two differently shaped cooking spaces behave alike.

First, define your usual portion

Write down a typical meal and how many people you usually serve. Do you cook a side dish, a full meal or snacks? Do you mind preparing food in multiple rounds? A clear answer to these questions is more useful than an abstract “large enough” label.

Then check these five details

  1. Cooking surface: Find the usable basket or tray dimensions where the manufacturer provides them.
  2. Loading guidance: Check the manual for recommended amounts and whether food should sit in a single layer.
  3. Batch rhythm: Account for the time and attention needed to cook a second round.
  4. Food shape: A bulky item may use space differently from smaller pieces. Consider your own regular ingredients.
  5. Household routine: Think about whether everyone eats together or separate batches would be practical.

Questions worth asking

  • What meal would I cook first, and for how many people?
  • Will the food I cook need to be turned, shaken or spaced apart?
  • Does the official manual describe capacity in a way I can compare?
  • What will I do if the cooking surface is crowded?
  • Will the appliance fit the worktop and storage space I measured?
Do not compare the headline number alone

Look for comparable units and exact model documentation. If usable dimensions or recommended quantities are not confirmed, leave them unknown rather than estimating.

Keep a fair comparison

Use the same meal, household size and preparation assumptions for every candidate. Record the source and date for each specification. If a detail comes from a retailer listing, confirm it against the manufacturer or official product documentation before treating it as settled.
