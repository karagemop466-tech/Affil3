Black Friday kitchen deals: a checklist before you click add to cart

Deal week runs on urgency. Your decision should not. A short, reusable checklist for judging a kitchen discount before the countdown timer starts.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Deal week is loud on purpose. Every appliance on every site wears a percentage-off badge, and the banners blink in the exact rhythm that makes deciding feel like running out of time. Here is the checklist we keep taped to the inside of a cupboard door. It works in November and it works in any other month.

Decide your number first

A deal is only good relative to the price you would actually pay. Before you open a single listing, decide what this thing is worth to you. A discount off a number you would never have paid is not a saving; it is arithmetic. Write your number down, then shop against it.

Read which offer the price belongs to

A listing can carry several offers at once: new from the brand, new from a reseller, used, warehouse. A lower number attached to a used or warehouse offer is not a discount on the new one, and the headline badge rarely makes the distinction for you. Look inside the offer box before you believe the banner.

Price the whole ownership, not just the box

  • Replacement parts. A unit whose baskets or trays are sold separately has a running cost; the listing or the manual usually says what is available.
  • Accessories you will actually use. Bundles change the unit math, and the per-unit figure is usually printed right on the listing if you look for it.
  • Counter space. The best-priced appliance is a bad deal if it lives on the floor because it does not fit under the cabinet. The dimensions are on each product page on this site, in the specification grid.
  • Your time. Two small baskets finish dinner differently than one big one; so does an oven with three rack levels. The cheapest shape is the one that matches how you actually cook.

Check the return window before the deal, not after

Return policies stretch and shrink with the season, and they vary by item and by seller. Read the return window on the listing before you buy, while you can still walk away. Impulse buys are safer with a generous window, but only if the item actually qualifies for it.

Ignore the countdown

A timer is merchandising, not information. It tells you what the seller wants you to feel, not what the item is worth. If the deal disappears while you are thinking, the honest question is not whether you missed out, but whether you would have bought it at your number in the first place.

Why this site never shows you a price

Prices change hourly, and Amazon's own rules say a site may display price and availability only from data Amazon serves or supplies through its authorized tools. This site does neither, so it shows no prices anywhere. Every button on this site goes straight to the live listing, where the current number always is, with the day's date on it rather than a stale one in our copy.

  1. Write down your number before you browse.
  2. Check which offer the price belongs to.
  3. Price the parts, the accessories and the counter space.
  4. Read the return window while you can still walk away.
  5. Treat every countdown timer as decoration.

Questions readers ask next

Is a percentage-off badge proof of a real discount?

No. It is a merchandising label. Only comparison against the recent price tells you whether the number moved, and only your own budget tells you whether it matters.

Should I wait for the next deal day instead?

If waiting costs you nothing and you would buy at your number either way, waiting is free. If the item is for a specific meal or a gift with a date, a smaller honest discount beats a hypothetical bigger one.

Do your buy buttons change during deal week?

No. They always go to the live listing on Amazon with our tracking ID, deal week or not. Whatever price is showing there when you click is the real one.
