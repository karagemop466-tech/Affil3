Air fryer presets: how many do you actually need?

Four presets and twenty-five presets are both fine. The question is how many of them are foods you already cook.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

A preset button is a saved set of time and temperature. It is convenient when it saves you a decision and mildly annoying when it does not. The number of presets tells you almost nothing about the machine; the list of presets tells you almost everything.

The count is a marketing number

Twenty-five sounds like versatility. Four sounds like a compromise. Both readings are wrong, because a preset is only useful if it matches a food you cook on a Tuesday. Count the foods you would actually press the button for. For most households that number is somewhere between three and six: chicken wings, fries, frozen vegetables, a piece of fish, and leftovers.

The big oven is not competing on the same axis

This is the part the spec sheet hides. The 26 qt unit is described by its maker as a 10-in-1 air fry convection oven with 25 cooking pre-sets and a dual-speed fan (S13). It is standing in for a full oven: a single cavity with a door, racks and more than one level. The 8 qt stainless unit is a single drawer with four presets, a 200–400 °F range and a top-rack dishwasher-safe basket (S22). They are not competing on buttons. They are competing on what you can cook at once.

Dual baskets replace presets with independence

The 6 qt dual-basket unit takes a different route again. Instead of a long preset list, it gives you a Sync Finish function that ends both baskets at the same moment and a Hi-Fry setting that reaches 450 °F (S11). Two foods, two timers, one cycle. For a household cooking two different dinners, that is worth more than twenty-five buttons.

What to actually do with this

  • Count the foods you cook weekly, not the foods you could cook.
  • Check whether the presets match those foods, or whether they are named after dishes that sound impressive.
  • Decide whether you want help with time and temperature, or with running two things at once. Those are different features.
  • Treat a preset as a starting point, not an instruction. Every appliance runs differently, and a preset on a 450 °F Hi-Fry machine is not the same cook as a preset on a 200–400 °F machine.

Questions readers ask next

Are more presets better?

No. More presets is more buttons. The useful count is how many presets match food you already cook weekly.

Do I need the highest temperature?

Only if you brown things. The tracked units we list span a 200–400 °F range up to a 450 °F Hi-Fry setting, and Hi-Fry on the dual-basket unit is a short, hot finish rather than the whole cook.

Can I just use the manual settings?

Yes, and many people do. Presets are a convenience for repeat dishes; time and temperature on the dial give you more control, and they are what you will reach for with anything new.
