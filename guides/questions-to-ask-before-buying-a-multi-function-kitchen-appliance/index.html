Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

A 10-in-1 badge is a list of functions, not a promise that you will use them.

By Affil3 editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Multi-function appliances trade specialisation for range. That is a good trade if the functions you need are the ones your routine actually uses, and a bad one if the extra functions are what you pay for and never touch.

Questions that separate the useful functions from the badge

  1. Which three functions will you use in a normal week? Write them down before you read any listing.
  2. Does the maker list the functions individually? The tracked Emeril oven names ten: air fry, bake, broil, defrost, dehydrate, grill, proof, reheat, roast and warm.
  3. Do the functions need extra accessories to work? Rotisserie needs a spit; grilling may need a plate sold separately.
  4. Is the preset count consistent? The same Emeril page states 25 presets in its specification tiles and 24 in its description. When a maker disagrees with itself, treat the count as approximate.
  5. Does one function compromise another? A big oven takes longer to preheat for a small batch than a drawer does.

The badge versus the manual

Every maker's product page is a sales page. The manual is where the limits are stated: what temperature each function reaches, what accessories are required, what must not go in the dishwasher. If a manufacturer publishes the manual as a PDF on the product page, download it before you buy. The tracked products link their manuals where the maker provides one.

One worked example

The Emeril Lagasse French Door AirFryer 360 is sold as a 10-in-1 with 24 or 25 presets, depending on which part of its own page you read. It holds 26 quarts and measures 16.9 in deep by 15.8 in wide by 14.8 in tall. Its maker does not publish a wattage or a temperature range on the product page, so this site does not claim either. That absence is itself a question worth asking before you buy.

If you only ask one question

Ask what the appliance does badly. Every one of them does something badly: a 26-quart oven is slow to heat for a handful of fries, and a drawer with two 3-quart baskets cannot roast a whole chicken. Find that answer before you buy, not after.
