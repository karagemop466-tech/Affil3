Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Multi-function appliances promise a lot. Ask which functions you will actually use.

By Affil3 editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026 · Editorial policy

The short version

  • Multi-function appliances trade specialisation for counter space; decide which trade you want.
  • Count the functions you will use weekly, not the number printed on the box.
  • Check preheat time and the manual's function list before you commit.

Eight questions

  1. Which three functions will you use every week? Ignore the rest when comparing.
  2. Do the presets match the food you cook, or will you set time and temperature yourself?
  3. Can you see the temperature and time while the appliance runs?
  4. Does the manual include time and temperature charts for your foods?
  5. What does the warranty cover, and who services it? Read the warranty text rather than the summary.
  6. Are replacement baskets, racks or trays sold separately? Check the listing and the manufacturer's site.
  7. How loud is it? If the manufacturer gives no figure, note that you could not confirm it.
  8. Where will it live, and how often will you move it?

Why these questions

A list of functions is not the same as a list of things you will use. These questions tie the purchase to your routine and to the documents the manufacturer provides.

The Shop lists one multi-function air fryer and toaster oven we observed on 2026-11-20. We have not tested it.

What this guide does not claim

  • We have not tested any multi-function appliance on this site.
  • Preheat times, noise levels and power draw are not recorded here.
  • A function existing on the panel does not tell you how well it performs.

Questions buyers ask next

Do more presets mean better cooking?

Not necessarily. Presets are starting points. The manual and your own thermometer tell you more than the preset count.

Is a French-door oven easier to load than a drawer?

It depends on what you cook. Doors and racks suit trays and long items; a drawer suits shaking and tossing.
