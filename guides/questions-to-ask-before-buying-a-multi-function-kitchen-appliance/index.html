Questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Every function on the box is a function you have to store, clean and learn. Ask which ones you will actually use.

By Affil3 editorial team· Published November 20, 2026· Specifications checked November 20, 2026· 7 sections

A multi-function countertop appliance is a bet that one machine can replace three. Sometimes it can. The honest way to decide is to count the functions you will use weekly, then check whether the unit does those well, rather than counting the functions on the front panel.

1. Which functions do you already perform weekly?

Write down what you cooked in the last two weeks. If the list is mostly toast, frozen fries and reheating leftovers, a large multi-function oven is a lot of cabinet to buy for three jobs a much smaller appliance already does. If the list includes a whole chicken, a tray of vegetables and a bake on the same evening, the case gets stronger.

2. Does the manufacturer agree with itself?

The Emeril Lagasse 26 Qt page states '25 Cooking Pre-sets' in its specification strip and '24 one-touch cooking presets' in its own product description, and an image on the same page reads '11-in-1' (S13). The Amazon listing for the same unit says 24 presets and six accessories, while the manufacturer text mentions seven. When a manufacturer cannot keep one number straight across one page, treat the preset count as marketing rather than specification.

That is why this site publishes no preset count for this unit. The conflict is logged as FLG-005 with both source links, so you can look at the same pages and decide for yourself.

3. How much counter does it actually take, with the door open?

The unit is 16.9 inches deep, 15.8 wide and 14.8 tall by the manufacturer's figures (S13). Add the door swing and the clearance behind it for the cord. If your counter is 24 inches deep, you have about 7 inches left in front of the unit when it is pushed back, which is not enough room to slide a hot tray out safely.

4. Which parts go in the dishwasher?

For this unit, the manufacturer page does not state a dishwasher claim for any part (S13). If easy cleanup is the reason you are buying a multi-function appliance, that omission matters more than any preset.

5. What is the failure mode?

A single machine that does five jobs is also a single point of failure for five jobs. If it dies, you lose the toaster and the oven at once. This is not an argument against the category; it is an argument for knowing which of your other appliances you would keep in the cupboard as a backup.

6. Has anyone tested it, or is this a spec sheet?

Everything on this page is a specification comparison drawn from the manufacturer's own product page, not a test. This site has not cooked in this unit, has not measured how evenly it browns, and does not know how loud the fan is. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. The point of the specification approach is that you can check every number yourself against the source link.

If you only ask one question

Ask which two functions you will use at least once a week. If the answer is 'air fry and reheat', buy the smallest air fryer that fits your food and keep your oven. If the answer is 'air fry and bake, for six people', the big unit starts to make sense.
