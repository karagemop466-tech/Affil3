Which air fryer parts are actually dishwasher-safe?

Cleanup is the cost you pay every day, and it is the specification most listings treat as a footnote. Here is what each maker states, in their own words, and where they stop.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026 · 681 words, about a 3 minute read

The short answer

Only two of the three air fryers here publish a dishwasher claim, and both are narrow: the Chefman dual-basket listing calls its baskets nonstick and dishwasher safe, and the 8 qt Chefman page says basket and rack are top-rack dishwasher safe. The Emeril oven page states nothing about washing in the text this site reads, and ThermoMaven calls its probe 100% waterproof without mentioning a dishwasher, while CHEF iQ does state dishwasher-safe probes. A missing claim is not a permission: the parts nobody names are the parts to check in the manual.

Every buying guide tells you the capacity. Almost none of them tells you what you will be doing at 8pm with a greasy basket in your hand. So we took the one line that decides that - what the maker says may go in a dishwasher - and wrote down exactly what each official page states, including the pages that state nothing.

A missing claim is not permission

It is tempting to read the Emeril row above as “the accessories are hand-wash only”. It does not say that either. What it says is that the pages we read do not answer the question - and on a product whose pitch includes a stack of accessories that come in the box, that gap is worth asking about before the first dinner, not after. Where a page is silent, this site publishes silence.

The same applies to the parts no page mentions: the housing, the heating element, the crumb tray, the door glass. None of the pages we read states whether those can be wet, so none of them is described here. The manual is the only document that speaks for the machine you will actually receive.

Three questions worth answering from the manual

  1. Which component does the claim cover? “Basket” and “basket and rack” are different claims, and neither one includes the body of the machine.
  2. Is there a rack position? The 8 qt Chefman page says top rack. That qualifier is the manufacturer limiting its own claim, so treat it as part of the claim.
  3. Does the nonstick coating get its own sentence? All three air fryers here describe nonstick surfaces, and a coating can survive a dishwasher while the maker still recommends hand-washing to keep it. Those are two different statements.

The one number to double-check while you are there

Accessory count. The Emeril listing title says six cooking accessories are included, while the same listing's product summary mentions seven, and the manufacturer page states no count in the text we read. Neither number is published as a fact here; the disagreement is recorded and dated as FLG-024. It is a fair illustration of why this site reads pages instead of trusting a marketing line.

Nobody in this session put a basket in a dishwasher. Nothing on this page is a test result; it is a record of what five official pages state, with the date each was read. Two of them - the 8 qt Chefman page and the CHEF iQ page - were read in an earlier dated pass and are cited by their register ids; the other three were re-read on 2026-11-20 and say so in the source register.

Questions readers ask next

Is dishwasher-safe the same as waterproof?

No. A waterproofing statement is about water reaching the device; a dishwasher claim is about heat, detergent and spray specifically. ThermoMaven states the first and not the second, which is why its row reads the way it does.

Why does this site not just say “yes, all of them are fine”?

Because the pages do not say that, and a wrong answer here costs you a coating or a warranty. Every claim on the site has to point at an official page, and where the official page is silent the site has to be silent too.

What about baked-on grease?

That is not a manufacturer claim on any of these pages, so it is not answered here. This page answers one question - what the maker permits - not what a particular soaking routine can survive.

The specification grid on every product page carries the washing row, with “not stated” where a maker does not state it.
