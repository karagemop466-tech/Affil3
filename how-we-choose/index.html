Editorial policy

How this site decides what to say

Six rules. They are the reason some cells in our tables are empty.

1. A specification needs a manufacturer page

Capacity, wattage, dimensions and dishwasher claims come from the maker's own product page, fetched on the date shown next to the table. If the maker does not state it, the cell says “not stated”. We do not fill gaps from reseller listings or from other reviews.

2. We do not publish prices

Prices change hourly and Amazon's Program Policies only let a site display price data that Amazon itself serves or that comes through its API. We do neither, so every buy button says “check the current price on Amazon” and links out. S03

3. We do not publish star ratings

Ratings are a snapshot of a rolling average. Quoting one without the date and the sample is misleading, and keeping it current is a full-time job. Read them on the listing, where they are live.

4. We say what we have not tested

Nobody here has cooked in any of these appliances. Everything is a specification comparison. Where that leaves a real question open, such as how evenly a unit browns, we say so rather than inventing an answer. S07

5. When two official pages disagree, we publish neither number

Two of the makers on this site contradict themselves. Rather than pick the friendlier figure, both are recorded on the flagged for review page with the links, and the field stays blank.

6. Corrections are public

If a number here is wrong, it gets fixed and the fix gets logged, not quietly swapped. The route for that is on the About page.

The short version: a number you can check is worth more than a paragraph you cannot.
