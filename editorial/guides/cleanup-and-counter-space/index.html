How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

Published 2026-11-20 · written from manufacturer specifications, not from hands-on testing · how this site works

An air fryer that lives in a cupboard gets used half as often as one that lives on the counter. That is a specification problem, not a discipline problem.

Cleaning, by part count

A single-basket unit has one basket and one tray. A dual-basket unit has two of each. That is twice the washing for the ability to cook two foods at once — worth it if you use both zones, wasted effort if you usually use one.

Oven-style units add racks and a drip tray, and the interior walls need wiping. Check whether the manufacturer states dishwasher safety for each removable part; if it is stated for the basket but not the tray, treat the tray as hand-wash.

Counter space, measured honestly

Put a tape on the counter. Subtract the appliance depth. Subtract the clearance the door or drawer needs. What is left is your actual working space. Many kitchens have the footprint but not the clearance.

The under-cabinet test

Height plus the lid or door arc is the number that matters if the appliance sits under cabinets. A tall unit that fits the counter may not fit the gap.

What to do with this

Compare the depth and height rows on each product page here before anything else. They are the two rows a return is usually about.

This is a specification comparison, not a product test. Nobody here has used these appliances. The value is in reading the official documentation carefully and showing you where it is incomplete or contradictory — not in pretending to have cooked with them.
