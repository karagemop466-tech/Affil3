A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Published 2026-11-20 · written from manufacturer specifications, not from hands-on testing · how this site works

Quarts are the most quoted and least comparable number on an air fryer box. Here is how to turn it into something you can use.

Ask four questions about the same number

  1. Is it one chamber or several? Six quarts as two 3-quart baskets cannot hold what six quarts as one basket holds.
  2. What shape is the chamber? A tall narrow basket and a wide flat one hold different foods at the same volume.
  3. How many people is it for? The manufacturer states a serving range for some models and not others. Where it is stated, quote it; where it is not, do not invent it.
  4. Does the basket come out? Capacity you cannot lift out and wash is capacity you use once.

Read the dimension line, not the capacity line

Two 8-quart units can differ by more than an inch in every direction. The external dimensions decide whether it fits; the internal capacity decides whether the food fits. You need both, and most listings lead with only the second.

Where this site leaves a blank

If a manufacturer does not publish a serving range or a preset count, the row stays empty here and is marked unverified. An empty row is information: it means the number you saw elsewhere came from somewhere other than the manufacturer.

This is a specification comparison, not a product test. Nobody here has used these appliances. The value is in reading the official documentation carefully and showing you where it is incomplete or contradictory — not in pretending to have cooked with them.
