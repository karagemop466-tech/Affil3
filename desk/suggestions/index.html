Suggestions

Everything this desk would like to change, with the reason, the effort and the risk. Nothing here has been done on its own except where the build notes say it has. Read the paths first, then pick.

First, pick a path

The suggestions below assume a shape for the site. Four shapes are on the table and only one of them matches the five links that were supplied. This is the decision that everything else waits on.

PATH-A

One desk, two tools

Keep both categories on a single site: air fryers and kitchen thermometers, published as one brand with two category hubs.

Why it works
It matches the five supplied links exactly, so nothing in the brief has to be re-scoped. Both categories sit in Home and Kitchen on Amazon, and one of the supplied thermometers is listed for air fryer use, which gives the site a natural bridge between them.
Unlocks
  • Both category hubs have enough product to be worth a hub
  • A thermometer guide can send readers to an air fryer guide and vice versa
  • One brand, one disclosure, one ledger
Costs
  • Neither category gets the depth a specialist site would have
  • Search intent is split across two very different question sets
Best when
The owner wants the site to look like a publication quickly and is not yet sure which category will carry the traffic.

Recommended starting point. It is what the supplied links already describe.

PATH-B

Air fryer desk only

Drop the thermometers, build one deep air fryer desk with many more products and far more guides.

Why it works
It is the same site with one category removed, so nothing has to be rebuilt — only narrowed. The register, the disclosure and the ledger all keep working.
Unlocks
  • Deeper guides on a single question set, which is what search engines reward
  • A bigger register, which makes comparison pages worth their own category
Costs
  • Two of the five supplied links have no home on the site
  • The site starts over with a smaller product set
Best when
Measured traffic later shows that air fryer questions bring readers, and the air fryer category has a much larger commission or a better conversion rate.

Defer. Make it after the analytics have run for a season.

PATH-C

One product, one long page

A single deep page per product, with all the comparison content on it.

Why it works
It is the smallest possible build and the fastest way to find out whether anything you write brings a single click.
Unlocks
  • The fastest possible build
  • A very clear single click path to Amazon
Costs
  • Almost no search surface area, so organic traffic will stay thin
  • Hard to add a second product later without a redesign
  • Reads exactly like the kind of thin affiliate page the brief is trying to avoid
Best when
The goal is a link to send to a small audience, not to build a site.

Not recommended. It contradicts the brief's goal of organic traffic.

PATH-D

Kitchen appliance desk

Broaden to the whole small-appliance category: blenders, toasters, coffee, air fryers, thermometers.

Why it works
It matches the shape of Amazon's own catalogue and lets the guides follow real shopping journeys rather than one narrow decision.
Unlocks
  • A much larger register and a much larger keyword surface
  • A natural home for the cross-category buying guides
Costs
  • Every product needs its own verification and its own page, which multiplies the review burden
  • The site stops being about anything in particular
  • Amazon's Program Policies require additional original content for product-list pages, which gets expensive fast
Best when
A dedicated operator and a functioning Creators API make catalogue research cheap.

Not yet. Earn it with data first.

Recommended order

  1. Close Q-01 and Q-03. Both are inside the owner's own account and take minutes.
  2. Apply the chosen path from the four above, then publish the first season of guides on it.
  3. Let the analytics endpoint and Search Console run for a season before choosing the next topic.
  4. Then pick up the blocked items in the order of their dependency.
