Instrument

Measurement

Five events, one collector we run ourselves, no cookies, and an explicit refusal to record anything a bot could fire. The point is not vanity numbers: it is that the site should be able to say which paragraph earned a click, and then behave as if that answer exists.

Endpoint status, stated plainly

The endpoint is null. GitHub Pages serves static files and cannot run a process, so as published this site records nothing about its visitors; the script counts in the visitor’s browser and goes no further. Deploying tools/collect_server.py on any host that runs a process turns measurement on by writing one value into data/program.json. An empty ledger is a legitimate result. A filled one that nobody measured is not.

The five events

EV-01 · page_view

Fires on
the page's own script, after first paint, once per load
Extra fields
host_kind (pages|local)
What it answers
Did anyone arrive, and on which page?
Deliberately not recorded
referrer string, user agent, screen size, IP
Why it is limited that way
Referrer is deliberately dropped: it is the only field in this design that could identify a person's browsing, and the value of keeping it is smaller than the cost of disclosing it.

EV-02 · guide_view

Fires on
a guide or topic page, when the reader has been visible to the page for 30 seconds or has scrolled past 75%, whichever comes first
Extra fields
doc, dwell_s, depth_pct
What it answers
Is the editorial work being read, or bounced from?
Deliberately not recorded
mouse movement, keystrokes, exact scroll trace
Why it is limited that way
A threshold instead of a continuous trace keeps it a reading signal rather than surveillance, and 'engaged' means something defensible: 30 seconds is stated in the schema, so it cannot quietly be redefined later to flatter a page.

EV-03 · outbound_click

Fires on
pointer activation on an element marked data-affiliate, before navigation
Extra fields
product_slot, destination_host, tag_present, block_id
What it answers
Which shelf card or which paragraph turned a reader into a click - the only on-site signal that has a commission attached to it.
Deliberately not recorded
the final URL, the query string, anything after the host
Why it is limited that way
tag_present is a boolean, not the tag value, so the event never carries account credentials anywhere they do not belong. Nothing is sent to Amazon; the click itself is what Amazon measures, and its report is the only conversion source we trust [S18].

EV-04 · disclosure_view

Fires on
the disclosure strip entering the viewport on a page that contains at least one outbound link (IntersectionObserver; no timer, no synthetic firing)
Extra fields
page_kind
What it answers
Does the disclosure actually appear before the click, which is the claim the site makes about itself?
Deliberately not recorded
whether the reader read it - nobody can measure that honestly
Why it is limited that way
This is a placement instrument, not a legal defence. Seeing a strip is not comprehension, and no report should ever claim that the ratio above satisfies the FTC standard on its own.

EV-05 · product_intent_confirmation

Fires on
the local collector, when tools/product_intake.py confirm writes a confirmation record (so the shelf's go-live trail is time-stamped by a server, not by an editor's claim)
Extra fields
slot, method, confirmation_date, actor
What it answers
When did a product become shoppable, on what evidence, and who said so?
Deliberately not recorded
nothing extra is needed; the durable copy is the git commit of data/products.json
Why it is limited that way
The register is the truth; this event is only its clock. Do not treat it as proof of a buyer's consent or of the listing's accuracy.

Running the collector

python3 tools/collect_server.py --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8099 \ --out data/ledger/events.jsonl --origin karagemop466-tech.github.io

Stdlib only. Accepts one JSON object per POST, validates it against the schema above, rejects unknown event names and any payload over 2 KB, and does not read or store the peer address. Summarise with python3 tools/events.py report.

What this cannot tell you

Not revenue, not orders, not a person. Conversions live in Amazon's reports and arrive only through the path described in the ledger; a click here that produced nothing may still produce an order for 89 days. And the traffic is currently near zero, so read any early count as an anecdote — the tooling labels it that way rather than letting a chart imply significance. [S18]
