Why nothing here is clickable yet

Three sentences, then the detail.

We were given five links in the form https://link.amazon/<9-character id>. From the machine that builds this site there is no route to that host, so we could not see where any of them land, what the product is called, or whether a tracking tag survives the hop. Publishing "Buy now" buttons over that unknown would put a reader one click from a 404 and put the account one step away from links that do not attribute.

So the shelf publishes the state instead of the sale, and one person with the enrolled account can turn it on. The programme rules make this a money question, not a style one: purchases arriving through a redirect that hides the intermediate step are disqualified, and so is anything whose link formatting fails to track.

The five-minute version

  1. Sign in to the Amazon Associates account, open each submitted link, and read the final URL.
  2. Check the address bar contains tag=shopnow047c-20. Do not buy anything to test it — self-purchase through your own links is prohibited.
  3. Record the outcome: python3 tools/product_intake.py confirm --slot 1 --observed-redirect '<final url>' --title '<listing title>' --method SiteStripe --date <YYYY-MM-DD>
  4. Check the next-day report shows the click, then flip commerce_switch.allow_live_links and rebuild. Each card then goes live individually, because each has its own link_state.

Full protocol: docs/LINK_TEST_PROTOCOL.md. What it enforces automatically: the gate.
