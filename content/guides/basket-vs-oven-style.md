## The question nobody answers for you

Almost every air fryer decision gets framed as brand versus brand. That is the wrong fork. The right fork is *shape*: a basket you pull out and shake, or a cabinet with a door you open like an oven.

Everything else - capacity, cleanup, counter footprint, what you can cook on a Tuesday - falls out of that one choice.

An air fryer is a small convection oven. A fan moves hot air across the food fast enough that the surface dries and browns sooner than a full-size oven manages. That is the whole trick. Because the cavity is small, the fan has an unusually strong effect, and the geometry inside the cavity matters more than most spec sheets admit.

## Four questions that settle it

**1. What is the widest thing you will actually cook?**

A basket is round and shallow. A whole hen, a 9x13 pan, a rack of ribs, a slice of pie with a plate under it - those are oven-style problems, not capacity problems. People buy a big-round-basket fryer and then discover the basket is 8 inches across and 3 inches deep, and that a dinner plate does not fit.

Measure the thing you most want to cook. Then measure the opening, not the advertised volume. The opening is the constraint.

**2. Do you cook in batches or in one go?**

Baskets win batches: one batch, shake it, second batch, everything comes out at eating temperature because the second batch started while you were plating the first. Dual-basket models split the cavity so two foods finish together - the point is synchronising, not doubling capacity.

Oven-style wins single large items and wins anything that needs a rack, a tray, or a door you can leave cracked.

**3. Where does it live?**

If it goes back in a cupboard every time, the basket wins on friction, and friction decides what you actually use. If it stays out, then you are buying a counter appliance and you should price the counter space honestly - see [the cleanup and counter-space guide](/guides/cleanup-and-counter-space/) before you commit to a cabinet.

**4. Do you want the other functions, or do you want the one function well?**

Oven-style machines are usually sold as 6-in-1 or 8-in-1: air fry, roast, bake, dehydrate, toast, sometimes proof. Sometimes that is a real replacement for a toaster oven and a dehydrator. Sometimes it is the same convection fan with a brochure attached. The [multi-function question set](/guides/multi-function-appliance-questions/) is how to tell which one you are looking at.

## What the shape costs you

| | Basket | Oven-style |
|---|---|---|
| Easiest to shake and flip | Yes | No - you open a door, use tongs |
| Largest single item | Limited by round, shallow footprint | Usually a rack and a tray |
| Even browning without attention | Requires shaking or turning | Fan plus rack does more of it for you |
| Cleanup surface | Coated basket, usually dishwasher-safe check your listing | Glass door, racks, crumb tray, more parts |
| Counter footprint | Small but tall-ish | Wide and deep |
| Preheat | Short | Longer cavity to fill |
| Noise character | Fan, close to ear height on a counter | Fan, often louder at the same distance |

The table is a set of *questions to check on a listing*, not a ranking. Nothing here says which is better for you, because that depends on the two numbers only you know: what you cook most, and how much counter you are willing to lose.

## The 90-second check at the listing

Open the product images and look for three things a marketing title will not tell you:

- **A photo with a hand or a spoon in it.** That is the only honest scale reference in the whole listing.
- **The interior dimensions, not the exterior ones.** Some pages give both; some give only the box size. If only the box size appears, you are guessing.
- **The basket or tray in a dishwasher.** If the listing says dishwasher-safe, read whether it says *top rack*, and whether the coating is called out as the reason to be careful.

Then read the review filter for *size* and *cleaning*, not for taste. Nobody complains that the wings were under-seasoned. They complain that the basket is small and the coating scratched.
