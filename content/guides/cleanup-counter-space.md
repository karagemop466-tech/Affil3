<p class="lede">Reviews talk about crisping. Daily life talks about the pile of parts in the sink and the appliance that slowly migrates to a cupboard. Cleanup and counter space are where kitchen gear gets honestly judged — and they're on every official listing, if you know where to look.</p>

<h2>What will you actually wash?</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Basket units:</strong> typically a basket plus a tray or crisper plate. Two or three parts, most with nonstick coatings, usually listed as dishwasher-safe. Fast.</li>
  <li><strong>Oven-style units:</strong> racks, trays, a drip pan, sometimes a rotisserie spit and forks. More parts — but also more of a "reset the whole machine" job, not a daily one, if you line the crumb tray.</li>
  <li><strong>Don't forget the cavity.</strong> Grease finds the walls and the heating element area on every air fryer. A model whose door opens wide and whose interior is smooth will be cleaned more often than a clever one that isn't.</li>
</ul>

<p>On the listings we've verified, "dishwasher-safe baskets" appears as an explicit claim for the <a href="../../shop/">6-quart dual-basket</a> and the <a href="../../shop/">8-quart basket model</a>; the <a href="../../shop/">oven-style unit</a> lists six included accessories, which is six things to wash or store. Checkboxes on a listing are claims from the seller — but they're checkable claims, and better than adjectives.</p>

<h2>Measure the counter, not the photo</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Get a tape measure out now.</strong> Width, depth, and — for oven-style units with a drop-down or French door — the clearance in front and above.</li>
  <li><strong>Check the cord exit and the exhaust direction.</strong> A unit that vents hot air at the wall will live somewhere other than the spot you planned.</li>
  <li><strong>Think in two states: in-use and stored.</strong> Some basket units fit under a cabinet; almost no oven-style unit works under a low cupboard while running. Heat needs headroom.</li>
  <li><strong>Weight matters more than people expect.</strong> The appliance you'll genuinely leave out is one you can move one-handed when you need the space back.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Noise, smell and the rest of "living with it"</h2>

<p>Every air fryer is a fan at close range. There's no silent category leader — but listings and manuals do state decibel figures occasionally, and "how loud in an open-plan apartment" is a better question than "is it quiet." For smell, the grease filter (or lack of one) is the spec that matters. For safety, look for auto-shutoff and cool-touch exteriors, which appear explicitly in the <a href="../../shop/">register products' own listing titles</a>.</p>

<h2>A five-minute decision score</h2>

<p>Score each candidate 1–5 on four rows: parts per cook, total counter footprint, storage friction, and noise tolerance. Total the rows. This isn't science — it's a forcing function so the crispiness spec on the box doesn't talk over the parts that will decide whether you still use the thing in March.</p>
