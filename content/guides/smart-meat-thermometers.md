<p class="lede">A wireless meat thermometer is a genuinely useful tool: you see the roast's temperature from the sofa instead of opening the oven every ten minutes. It's also a category full of app promises. Here's what's worth checking before you buy — including whether you need "smart" at all.</p>

<h2>What makes a probe worth owning?</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Heat tolerance, stated as a number.</strong> This is the spec that decides where the probe can live. Some probes are rated to extreme temps — one listing in our register claims 1000°F heat safety — while cheaper probes top out where grills and broilers live. A number on the listing beats an adjective.</li>
  <li><strong>Probe thickness.</strong> Ultra-thin probes leave smaller holes and read faster. It's a concrete claim listings make when it's true.</li>
  <li><strong>Probe count and placement.</strong> Two or more probes let you track two foods, or one food in two places — the difference between "the chicken is done" and "the whole chicken is done."</li>
  <li><strong>Accuracy statement.</strong> Look for a stated accuracy range or a certification claim. If the listing states none, assume none.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How smart does it need to be?</h2>

<p>Connectivity is a spectrum: instant-read (no radio), Bluetooth (backyard range), and WiFi (house-and-garden range). The <a href="../../shop/">unit in our register</a> is a WiFi model with multiple sensors and a charging base. Ask of any connected device:</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Does it work when the app doesn't?</strong> The thermometer must still show temperature on a base or screen. Apps get abandoned; food still needs cooking.</li>
  <li><strong>What is the battery story?</strong> Probe charging time, base battery, and whether the probes are rechargeable or disposable-cell.</li>
  <li><strong>What does the app ask for?</strong> Account creation, notifications, network access. A kitchen tool shouldn't need your contacts.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Who is this actually for?</h2>

<p>If you cook roasts, whole birds, or anything on a grill where "open the lid" costs you, a probe pays for itself in certainty alone. If you mostly cook ground meat and pasta, a $15 instant-read thermometer does 90% of the job with zero apps. The smart tier is for people who cook low-and-slow or multitask across rooms — be honest about which group you're in before paying the connected premium.</p>

<p>Stated max heat · probe count and thickness · accuracy claim · works without the app · battery and charging · warranty length.
