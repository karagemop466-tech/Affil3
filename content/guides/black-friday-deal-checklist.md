<p class="lede">It's deal week, and every appliance on every site is wearing a percentage-off badge. Some of those discounts are real. Some are arithmetic on a number nobody ever paid. Here's how we think about it — including on this site, where every price we show is stamped with the day we observed it.</p>

<h2>What is a "list price," really?</h2>

<p>Amazon states that the List Price shown on a listing is provided by the manufacturer, supplier or seller, and that displaying one depends on purchase and offer history over roughly the past 90 days — with the explicit caveat that list prices "may not necessarily reflect the product's prevailing market price" (<a href="https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GQ6B6RH72AX8D2TD" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Amazon's List Price help page</a>). In other words: the crossed-out number is context, not history.</p>

<h2>The six checks</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Compare against the last 30–90 days, not the crossed-out number.</strong> Price-history tools exist, but even a plain memory check ("what did this cost in October?") defeats most fake urgency.</li>
  <li><strong>Read which offer the price belongs to.</strong> Listings can show several sellers and conditions. A used-like-new price sitting next to a new price is not a discount on the new one — we observed exactly that combination on a listing in our register this week.</li>
  <li><strong>Check the return window before the deal, not after.</strong> Holiday return policies (many items bought in November–December 2026 can be returned through January 31, 2027) make impulse buys less risky — but confirm the item qualifies.</li>
  <li><strong>Price the parts too.</strong> A cheap unit with $35 replacement baskets isn't cheap. The manual or listing usually says what's available separately.</li>
  <li><strong>Watch the "per count" trick.</strong> Multi-packs and bundles change the unit math. The per-unit figure is printed right on the listing if you look.</li>
  <li><strong>Set your number first.</strong> Decide what the thing is worth to you before the countdown timer starts. The deal is only good relative to the price you'd actually pay.</li>
</ol>
