<p class="lede">Ignore the model names for a minute. Every air fryer on the market is one of three shapes, and each shape is a bet about how your household eats. Find your pattern below and the shortlist narrows without a single "editor's choice" badge.</p>

<h2>Shape one: the single basket</h2>

<p><strong>The bet:</strong> one food, done exceptionally conveniently, in batches if needed. Best for households of one to three, snacks, sides, and people upgrading from a microwave for reheating.</p>
<ul>
  <li>Strengths: fastest preheat-to-eating path, fewest parts to clean, smallest footprint of the three.</li>
  <li>Limits: one temperature at a time; mains and sides mean batches.</li>
  <li>In our register: the <a href="../../shop/">8-quart basket unit</a> — one roomy basket, 4-in-1 modes, dishwasher-safe parts, and a straightforward one-dial kind of workflow.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Shape two: the dual basket</h2>

<p><strong>The bet:</strong> two foods, two zones, one finish time. Best for households that always cook a protein plus a side — and for anyone who refuses to orchestrate batch timing on a weeknight.</p>
<ul>
  <li>Strengths: independent zones (different temps), sync-finish features that land both baskets together, and true separation of flavors.</li>
  <li>Limits: each basket is smaller than a single big one; two things to wash; the machine rarely does one huge batch well.</li>
  <li>In our register: the <a href="../../shop/">6-quart dual-basket unit</a> with two independent 3-quart baskets and a sync-finish mode — the shape in its clearest form.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Shape three: the oven-style air fryer</h2>

<p><strong>The bet:</strong> it becomes the kitchen's second oven. Best for bigger households, people who bake or roast often, and cooks who think in trays and racks.</p>
<ul>
  <li>Strengths: flat cook area (pizza, sheet-pan meals), multiple racks, many modes beyond air frying.</li>
  <li>Limits: the biggest footprint, longest preheat, most parts, and — worth saying plainly — it's a countertop oven, so it competes with your toaster and your real oven.</li>
  <li>In our register: the <a href="../../shop/">26-quart French-door unit</a> with 24 presets and six accessories — the shape at its most ambitious.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The map</h2>

<ol>
  <li>"I cook for one or two and want it <em>now</em>" → single basket.</li>
  <li>"Dinner is always two things at once" → dual basket.</li>
  <li>"I'd use it for baking, roasting, and holidays too" → oven-style.</li>
  <li>"I want all three" → buy shape one first; the resale market for barely-used air fryers is a graveyard of grand plans.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What the shapes have in common</h2>

<p>Every one of them: moves hot air fast, cooks best in a single layer, is judged by its parts and cleanup, and is priced across a wide range where paying more buys capacity and features — not a different cooking technology.
