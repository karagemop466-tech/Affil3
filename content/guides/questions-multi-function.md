<p class="lede">The pitch is always the same: one machine replaces five. Sometimes it's true. Often you get one machine that does two things well, three things grudgingly, and arrives with a manual thicker than the warranty. Here are the questions that sort one outcome from the other.</p>

<h2>Which functions will you actually use in a normal month?</h2>

<p>Be brutal. "Dehydrate" sounds life-changing until you run it for eleven hours once. List the meals you cooked last month and map them to functions. The honest set is usually two or three: air fry, bake, reheat, maybe roast. A <a href="../../shop/">unit in our register</a> advertises 24 one-touch presets; presets are recipes stored in a chip. They cost little and add convenience — but they are not 24 distinct capabilities.</p>

<h2>What does "replaces my toaster oven" mean in your kitchen?</h2>

<p>If the appliance takes over toast duties, it will run twice a day every day. Preheat time, door mechanics and crumb cleanup stop being specs and become quality-of-life. If it doesn't replace the toaster, it needs its own permanent counter home — revisit the <a href="../cleanup-counter-space/">counter-space checklist</a>.</p>

<h2>Which claims can the listing substantiate?</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Temperature range:</strong> e.g., a "450°F Hi-Fry" finishing mode (as one register listing states) is a concrete, checkable claim. "Crispier results" is marketing and should be weighted accordingly.</li>
  <li><strong>Accessories included:</strong> count them, and check whether they're needed for the functions you want (rotisserie forks only matter if you'll rotisserie).</li>
  <li><strong>Warranty length:</strong> it's in the listing or the manual. It's the cheapest proxy for how long the maker expects the electronics to last.</li>
  <li><strong>Smart features:</strong> if a thermometer or app is part of the pitch (our register includes a <a href="../../shop/">WiFi meat thermometer</a>), ask what happens when the app is abandoned. The probe should still work as a probe.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What's the true cost beyond the sticker price?</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Replacement parts.</strong> Nonstick baskets and trays eventually wear. Do they sell separately, and what do they cost?</li>
  <li><strong>Counter rent.</strong> The machine you keep out all year "costs" square footage daily.</li>
  <li><strong>Overlap with what you own.</strong> A toaster oven with an air-fry mode and a basket air fryer on the same counter is one machine too many for most kitchens.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The short pre-purchase interview</h2>

<p>Before checkout, answer out loud: What will I cook in it this week? Which functions overlap with something I already own? What am I giving up on the counter? What will I do when the coating wears? If those four answers still make you want it, the multi-function math works. If two answers make you hesitate, the feature list is selling you, not the other way round.</p>
