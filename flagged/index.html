Flagged for review

What we could not confirm

Irregularities, conflicts between two official sources, and values that could not be confirmed. Each is published on the Flagged for review page with its source links and the manual step that would close it. Flags never block publication; they tell a reader what not to trust yet. Numbering is continuous across sessions, and a resolved flag is kept rather than deleted so the audit trail survives. FLG-030 to FLG-035 were added by session 66f2548a on 2026-11-20 after re-reading the Operating Agreement and the Program Policies verbatim; numbering continues from main's FLG-029 because this branch had independently allocated FLG-023 to FLG-034 and those numbers were taken. Three findings from that earlier allocation were dropped rather than renumbered because main had already fixed them: the CI workflow that diffed a non-existent docs/ directory, robots.txt advertising two sitemaps over three crawlable publications, and the empty 'unresolved links' category page. Two more were dropped as duplicates of FLG-024 and FLG-025. FLG-036 was added and resolved by session 66f2548a on 2026-11-20; FLG-015 was resolved in the same pass because the site shipped imagery after the flag said it had none. Both keep their original wording so the register shows what changed rather than pretending it never did.

Nothing here blocks the site. Each item is a gap between what a reader might assume and what we can actually show. The manual step in each card is the fastest way to close it.

FLG-001 high Submitted tokens are 9 characters, not 10

All five submitted tokens (B04G1MEwY, B02NbDxp3, B00hJ57DV, B0c8moW59, B0f3GKalj) are 9 characters long. The observed amazon.com product identifiers in S04 are all 10 characters, and secondary sources describe ASINs as 10-character identifiers. No Amazon-owned definition was retrieved (see FLG-012). Because the intended ASIN cannot be confirmed, the intended ASIN, official listing URL, confirmation date and confirmation source are left blank for every row.

Sources: S04, link.amazon/B04G1MEwY, S12

What would close it: Confirm the intended product for each submitted link in Associates Central (or in the Amazon product page you intended) and supply the full 10-character ASIN or product URL.

Sources: S04, link.amazon/B04G1MEwY

FLG-003 high Compliance status of the supplied short links is unresolved

The short links are not claimed to be invalid. Associates Program Policies Participation Requirements (w) restrict a link-shortening service in a manner that makes it unclear that the link goes to an Amazon Site (S02). Whether the link.amazon short links fall within that restriction has not been determined. The site does not use the short links for outbound links; they appear only as source evidence. Added 2026-11-20: Amazon's Associates help (S13) states that Amazon full links and Amazon short links are supported in the OneLink setup, and that third-party shortened links are not. Participation Requirements 2(a) requires the Associates tag as a parameter in each Special Link (S02). None of the four observed product redirects contains a tag parameter (see products). Attribution of the link.amazon links to shopnow047c-20 therefore remains unconfirmed, and this flag stays open.

Sources: S02, link.amazon/B04G1MEwY, S13

What would close it: Check the supplied short links against Associates Program Policies (w) and the Associates tracking reports. Decide whether to keep them as evidence only or to replace them completely.

FLG-030 high Program Policies bar using Special Links or Program Content to develop machine learning models without Amazon's prior written approval

Participation Requirements §5, read verbatim 2026-11-20: "You will not, without or express prior written approval, use, or allow any third party to use, any Special Links or Program Content to develop machine learning models or related technology." The brief for this project asks for an AI-powered platform that automates product research, generates content and optimises campaigns. The clause does not forbid that outright - it is about using Special Links or Program Content as model input - but it draws a hard boundary around the automation the brief describes, and no earlier session had recorded it. Everything built so far stays inside it: no Amazon content was used to train or fine-tune anything, product facts were read from manufacturer and Amazon pages and cited by source ID, and outbound links are constructed from an observed ASIN plus the tracking ID rather than harvested at scale.

Sources: S02, S01

What would close it: Before adding automated scraping, model training or bulk link generation, ask Associates support whether it needs prior written approval under §5 and record the reply against this flag. Do not build the automated product-research pipeline until that answer exists.

FLG-031 high The Site may not have price tracking or price alerting functionality unless Amazon agrees

Participation Requirements §6(y), read verbatim 2026-11-20: "Unless otherwise agreed by Amazon, your Site must not have price tracking and/or price alerting functionality." The brief asks the platform to analyse pricing and to track conversions. Recording prices for internal analysis is a different act from shipping a price-tracking feature to readers, and only the second is barred by 6(y) - but the distinction has to be chosen deliberately rather than arrived at by accident. Related and stricter: §2(b) allows the Site to show prices and availability only where Amazon serves the link or the data comes from the Creators API or PA API, and §6(t) bars displaying customer reviews or star ratings without the same.

Sources: S02

What would close it: Decide explicitly: no price feature on the site (current, and the safe reading), or apply for PA API / Creators API access and get Amazon's agreement under 6(y) before building one. Any suggestion that would surface a price should be marked as needing this decision first.

FLG-032 medium The published site loads a click counter and carries no privacy notice

Sources: S02

What would close it: Review the /privacy/ page added by session 66f2548a. It describes what the counter does, what it never collects, why the endpoint is unset, and what would change if a third-party analytics product were adopted. It is a first draft of a legal-adjacent page written by a build session, not legal advice; Q-13 on the questions page stands.

FLG-033 low The tracking ID's shape does not match the example format in the policy, which is not the same as being wrong

Participation Requirements §2(b) gives the tag format as "XXXXX-##, or such other format as we may designate". The supplied ID shopnow047c-20 has an eleven-character prefix rather than five, and a two-digit suffix that does match. Because the clause expressly allows other designated formats, the shape proves nothing either way: it cannot confirm the ID is valid and it cannot show it is invalid. Recorded so that nobody later reads the eleven-character prefix as evidence of a problem, and so that the weaker argument - an Associates help page saying IDs end in -20 - is not mistaken for the policy text. Separately, §1(g) bars any Amazon trademark or variant or misspelling in a tag or Associates ID; shopnow047c contains none, which is a genuine pass.

Sources: S02, S03

What would close it: Export the tracking-ID list from Associates Central and confirm shopnow047c-20 appears on it. That closes this flag and FLG-011 together.
