The number that decides when to pull it: thermometer basics

Guessing at doneness is the most expensive habit in a kitchen. A thermometer turns it into a number.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Recipes give a time. Time is a hint, not a guarantee: it changes with the thickness of the cut, the heat of the oven and the starting temperature of the food. The number that actually decides whether something is done is its internal temperature, and the only reliable way to read that number is a thermometer.

The safe minimums to aim for

The US Department of Agriculture publishes a Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart. These are the targets it lists, and they are the numbers to compare a thermometer reading against.

FoodSafe minimum internal temperature
Beef, pork, veal, lamb (steaks, chops, roasts)145 degrees F, plus a 3-minute rest
Ground meats160 degrees F
All poultry, including ground poultry165 degrees F
Uncooked ham145 degrees F, plus a 3-minute rest

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart. We reproduce the chart's figures and do not add our own.

Where to put the probe

The USDA's food-thermometer guidance is equally specific about placement, because a reading taken in the wrong spot is a wrong reading. For whole poultry, insert the probe into the thickest part of the thigh, the wing and the breast, and avoid touching bone, fat or the pan.

  • Thickest part of the meat, not the edge.
  • Away from bone, fat and the cooking vessel, which conducts heat differently.
  • For whole poultry, check the thigh, the wing and the thickest part of the breast.

The two thermometers we track

We track two thermometers, and they do two different jobs. The ThermoMaven 1 Sec Professional is a folding instant-read probe built for quick spot checks; the CHEF iQ Sense is a wireless leave-in probe built to stay in the meat and report to an app. Both are grounded in their makers' published pages, cited on each product page.

Questions readers ask next

Is a thermometer really necessary?

For anything where doneness and safety meet, yes. A target internal temperature is checkable; a time is not.

Where do these temperature figures come from?

The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart and its food-thermometer guidance. We reproduce them and cite the source.
