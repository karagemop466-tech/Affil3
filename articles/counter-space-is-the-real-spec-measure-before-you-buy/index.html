Counter space is the real spec: measure before you buy

Capacity gets the attention. Footprint is the number that decides whether an appliance stays.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Every air fryer listing leads with capacity. The number that decides whether it stays on your counter is smaller and quieter: the case size, and the clearance the door or drawer needs when it is open.

The three cases, side by side

These are the manufacturers' own published case dimensions for the three basket-and-oven appliances we track. We publish them as the makers list them, without reordering or rounding them into a comparison we cannot support.

ProductCase dimensions (maker's order)Shape
Chefman 6 Qt Dual Basket13.38 x 11.81 x 10.63 inTwo baskets
Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt15.4 x 12.6 x 13.5 inSingle basket
Emeril Lagasse 26 QT16.9 x 15.8 x 14.8 inOven, french doors

Read the dimension order on the product page: the makers do not all label which figure is depth, width or height in the same way, so we reproduce their order rather than impose ours.

How to use these numbers

  1. Measure the counter space you actually have, including the clearance a swinging door or a pulled drawer needs.
  2. Check the largest dimension against that clearance, not the capacity number.
  3. Remember that a bigger case is not automatically better — it is a bigger commitment of the same space.

We do not rank these appliances. The dimensions above are the makers' published figures, cited per product, and no value here is inferred or filled in.
