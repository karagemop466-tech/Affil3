Two baskets, one machine: what dual-basket air fryers change

One appliance, two drawers, two timers. Here is what actually changes about a weeknight dinner.

By ShopNow Kitchen editorial team · Published November 20, 2026 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

A dual-basket air fryer sounds like a bigger air fryer. It is not. It is two smaller air fryers that happen to share one machine, one plug and one footprint. That distinction is the whole story, and it is the reason to pick one over a single-drawer model.

What a second basket changes

The unit we track is the Chefman 6 Quart Dual Basket Air Fryer. It carries 6 quarts in total, split into two independent 3-quart baskets. Each basket has its own nonstick surface and its own place in the cooking logic, so the two sides can be told to finish at the same moment — a feature the maker calls Sync Finish.

  • Two foods, two temperatures, two timings, in the space of one appliance.
  • Each basket has a window, so you can check one side without opening the drawer and letting the heat out.
  • The baskets are nonstick and dishwasher-safe, per the manufacturer.
  • The footprint is that of a single basket fryer, not two of them.

What to check before you buy

The number that decides whether a dual basket earns its place is not capacity. It is whether you actually cook two things at once. If your weeknight is one batch of fries and one protein, a dual basket removes the waiting-and-reheating step. If you routinely cook one large batch, two 3-quart drawers can feel like two half-measures.

Questions readers ask next

Is two baskets the same as a larger single basket?

No. Two 3-quart baskets hold the same total as one 6-quart drawer, but they let you run two different cooks at once. A single large drawer runs one cook and needs a shake halfway through.

Which is better for a large batch?

If you usually cook one big batch, a single large basket is the more direct fit. The dual basket earns its place when two things are on the menu at once.
