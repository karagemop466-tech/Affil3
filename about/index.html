About this desk

Who writes this, what the rules are, and what the desk refuses to do.

The desk

Editorial desk of Counter & Crumb. Product pages are assembled from the sources recorded in the product register; no product is described from memory.

Every page carries the same byline, every claim carries a link to the official page it came from, and every product attribute is quoted from an Amazon listing rather than described from memory. Where a fact could not be confirmed from an official source, it is left blank and flagged instead of being filled in.

Editorial standards

  1. A number we can link to is a number we print. Anything that cannot be re-checked at the source stays out.
  2. Blank beats plausible. An empty cell with a note is more useful than a guess that reads like a fact.
  3. Snapshots are labelled. Every observation carries the date it was made, because Amazon's listings change.
  4. Specs are attributed. Product specifications are quoted from the official listing title and marked as such. We do not present them as our own testing, because we have not tested the products.
  5. No popularity claims. Guide topics are working titles, not statements about what people search for. We will only say a topic is popular once a measurement says so.
  6. No earnings claims. No forecast, no conversion rate, no 'worth it' verdict on somebody else's money.
  7. Every link out is marked. Sponsored links carry rel sponsored nofollow, a visible marker and an aria-label, because a reader is entitled to know before they click.
  8. Recommendation over ranking. We say which product fits which situation instead of crowning a winner, because a winner depends on the reader.

What this desk will not do

  • Publish a price, a star rating or a review count without an API that supplies them lawfully.
  • Use an Amazon logo, a wordmark or a product photograph.
  • Publish a number it cannot source.
  • Present a guess as a measurement.

Privacy

This site's own collector stores no IP address, no cookie, no advertising identifier and no email address. It uses a random id that lives for one browser session, and it honours Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. The hosting platform is a different matter: GitHub's documentation states that visitor IP addresses are logged for security purposes regardless of consent, and this site says so rather than pretending otherwise. Full detail on the measurement page.
