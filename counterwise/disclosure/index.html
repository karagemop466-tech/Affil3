The money, plainly

Disclosure

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. That sentence appears under the masthead of every page on this site and again in the footer of every page, because Amazon requires that exact statement to appear clearly and conspicuously on the site, and because a reader should not have to hunt for the answer to "who pays you".

What that means in practice

  • Links to Amazon carry our Associates tag. If you click one and buy, Amazon pays this site a share of the sale from the seller. The price you pay does not change.
  • Every product link on the page carries a (paid link) label next to it, not a footnote pointing at one. Amazon's guidance is that a link-level disclosure should be clear — "(paid link)", "#ad" or "#CommissionsEarned" are its own examples — and conspicuous enough that readers notice it near the link.
  • Those links are marked rel="sponsored nofollow" so search engines read them as paid placements, which is what they are.
  • Nothing here is paid for by a brand. No sample has been received from any manufacturer on the register (Q-13), which is what makes the last point true.

What this site is not

It is not Amazon, it is not affiliated with the brands named in any listing, and it is not a review outlet: no product on these pages has been used by anyone here. Where the law and the program overlap — material connections under the FTC's Endorsement Guides at 16 CFR Part 255, the disclosure statement in Amazon's agreement — placement is the whole practical question, and placement is on every page rather than on an easy-to-miss page.

Reading the fine print yourself

Two pages we did not read in full this session and do not paraphrase: the Associates Operating Agreement, which renders inside a signed-in account, and the two FTC guidance pages Amazon's own help article points to. The gap is recorded as FLG-006 rather than papered over.
