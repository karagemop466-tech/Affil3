S-01 shippedPriority 1 — read like a shop design

Lead with buying help, not with the verification process

Proposal

Front page opens with a lead guide and a 'start here' question module, the four verified listings as a product rail below it, then clusters, then the seasonal note. The register, the gate report and the flag list move to a single footer group called Transparency.

Why

A buyer arrives with a question about dinner, not about our evidence chain. The register still has to be reachable, but it is a promise kept in the footer, not the front window.

Effect on readers

The first screen answers 'what should I compare'; the fifth screen proves we did not invent anything.

Cost · effort

none · done · risk low — the audit material is one click away, not removed · reversible yes

S-02 shippedPriority 5 — compliance gate compliance

Keep the disclosure above the fold on every page, including the front page

Proposal

A strip directly under the masthead on all pages carrying the required statement, a plain-English sentence about what that means for the reader, and a link to /disclosure/. The same statement repeats in the footer. Every product link carries '(paid link)'.

Why

Amazon's help page requires the statement clearly and conspicuously on the site and link-level disclosure near each link; the FTC rule behind it is about the reader noticing. Both are placement duties, and placement is checkable in build.

Effect on readers

Nobody has to scroll to the footer to learn we get paid, which is the single most trust-building thing an affiliate page can do.

Cost · effort

a 34px strip · done · risk if it were missing, the account is at risk; nothing about having it hurts · reversible no reason to

Depends on

nothing

Options
  1. Footer only — What most sites do; Amazon's help text explicitly says readers shouldn't have to hunt for it.
  2. Strip under the masthead plus footer, as shipped — Visible on every page load, one line of height. Chosen.
  3. Interstitial 'before you click' modal — Maximally conspicuous, measurably annoying, and it can suppress the clicks we are trying to earn.
Recommended

Strip plus footer, as shipped

S-03 needs-pickPriority 1 — read like a shop design

Type and palette: paper and ink, not the affiliate default

Proposal

Ship the system stack now (ui-serif display, system-ui body, three greys plus one paprika accent, no webfont request). When the domain question (S-15) is settled, self-host one display face and one text face, subset, preloaded.

Why

The current look reads generated because it uses the same three ingredients every template uses: a gradient, a card grid, and Inter. A serif with real type scale and one warm accent is the cheapest distance from that. Self-hosting rather than a CDN keeps the first paint on the critical path and adds no third party to a privacy page that promises no trackers.

Effect on readers

Faster first paint on a phone, and a page that looks like a title rather than a funnel.

Cost · effort

S-04 needs-pickPriority 3 — trustworthy machinery data

Product data: keep prices and ratings off the page until an authorized feed exists

Proposal

Publish identity, category and seller-titled attributes with capture dates, and let the marketplace show the live price. Revisit only when PA-API credentials exist (Q-05).

Why

A price copied from a listing page is a screenshot of yesterday. It goes stale silently, it undercuts trust the first time the reader sees a different number, and Google's product rich-result documentation ties presentation to exactly the fields we cannot keep current.

Effect on readers

Nobody is told a number we cannot stand behind.

Cost · effort

S-06 shipped-pending-proofPriority 2 — findable and functional compliance

Replace ambiguous outbound links with tagged Special Links, and prove one before scaling

Proposal

Rewrite every outbound link to https://www.amazon.com/dp/<observed-id>?tag=shopnow047c-20 with rel="sponsored nofollow", and mark each link pending-test in the UI until the in-account click report confirms it. Do not claim the supplied link.amazon forms are invalid; their status is FLG-003.

Why

Amazon's Special Links clause requires the tag as a URL parameter. Our capture of the four resolved destinations showed parameters for channel, button and language and no tag at all, which is a lost-commission shape regardless of what the policy says.

Effect on readers

Identical destination; the reader cannot tell and does not need to.

Cost · effort

  3. Keep the supplied link.amazon codes — Zero work, and every capture we took showed no tag at the destination.

Put a scale rule on page creation, in the gate

Proposal

Google's scaled content abuse policy targets many pages generated primarily to rank, however they are made — which is precisely the failure mode of an 'AI-powered affiliate platform'. Effect on readers
