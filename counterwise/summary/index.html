Executive summary

What exists, what is proven, what is waiting on one person

This repository is now a working affiliate publication with a verification gate in front of it. The site builds itself from a register of sources and evidence; a build fails when a page would claim more than the register supports. That inversion — automation for plumbing, a gate for claims — is the single design decision everything else follows from.

The catalogue

Five links were supplied. Four resolved to a readable product page on 2026-11-20: a twin-basket air fryer, a 26-quart French-door countertop oven, an 8-quart single basket, and a three-probe wireless meat thermometer. Together they describe one coherent shelf — countertop cooking, plus the instrument that makes it reliable — so the niche was adopted rather than invented. The fifth link never reached a listing from this environment and is published as an empty row with a reason. Every token in the five supplied links is nine characters, while the listings they opened carry ten-character identifiers; that arithmetic is why no ASIN is recorded as intended for any row.

What was verified against primary sources

Amazon's help text for the disclosure statement, the fixed commission table, Google's documentation of rel="sponsored" and of product structured data, the March 2024 spam policy on scaled content, the FTC Endorsement Guides as codified, and the USDA temperature chart. The full register — with what was read in full, what was captured as a snippet, and what was never read at all — is on the source page. A register that graded nothing would be decoration.

Two findings that change the plan

First, the tracking. None of the four resolved destinations carried a tag= parameter. As supplied, those links would have earned nothing, however much traffic arrived. The site therefore publishes rebuilt links with tag=shopnow047c-20, and the build refuses any Amazon link without it. The remaining step is small and human: prove one click per product in the reporting interface.

Second, the money is ambiguous and stays that way. The published fixed rates include Furniture, Home, Home Improvement, Lawn & Garden, Pets Products at 3.00%, and a row containing Kitchen at 1.25%. Air fryers sit under a Home & Kitchen breadcrumb, which decides nothing. A forecast built on either number would be fiction, so none is published anywhere on this site; the category is recorded from the first real report instead.

Reader-facing polish

Five question-shaped guides, three clusters, a comparison table in real HTML, disclosure above the fold on every page and repeated in the footer, artwork drawn for the site rather than lifted from a listing, an Atom feed, a sitemap, a written privacy page that matches the code, and an editorial voice enforced by the build instead of requested in a prompt. 11 of the 23 suggestions on the board are already shipped.

Measurement

The five-event schema exists, the click beacon is wired to it, and a stdlib collector is in the repository, deliberately unarmed: static hosting cannot run it, and an empty ledger is more useful than a plausible one. Nothing is labelled a conversion rate. The moment an endpoint is configured the ledger begins to fill from measured clicks, and only Amazon's own reports may add orders.

What is waiting on you

13 questions can only be answered by whoever holds the account — the intended listing per link, the existence and live test of the tracking ID, PA-API access, the Search Console property, and the marketplace boundary. Each carries the default this site applies while it stays open, so the project moves without waiting and no one has to invent an answer.

Where it stands: a published site with four listings, five guides, 16 registered sources, 17 open items shown on the review page, zero published prices or ratings, and one test in the Associates interface between this and scaling.
