Workflow

How a guide gets made

The pipeline from a reader question to a published, source-linked guide, the gates it passes on the way, and the topics actually queued.

The cadence, and why it is that slow

One guide every two weeks (suggestion S-05, option A), starting December 4, 2026. A guide at this site's standard needs a manufacturer page read in full, a listing read in full, and at least one conflict or gap written up honestly. That is a day of work, not an hour. A weekly cadence at that standard would produce the thin pages this site exists to avoid.

The cadence changes when a measured trigger fires, not when the schedule feels slow: Two consecutive four-week windows in which published guides average at least 50 outbound clicks a month from the site's own collector. Below that, more guides do not help; better ones do.

Topics are chosen like this: From Search Console query data once the property is verified (discovery task D-09), and from the questions readers actually ask before then. Not from intuition, and not from keyword-volume guesses this project cannot measure.

What this publication will not do

  • Best air fryers of 2026. A ranked list requires either testing every unit or reproducing someone else's ranking. This site does neither, and a ranking would force it to hide the trade-offs it currently publishes.
  • Amazon Prime Day deals roundup. It would require price data from an Amazon feed. The site publishes no prices by policy (S03, FLG-016), and a deals post that cannot show a price is worse than no post.
  • Anything generated in bulk to fill the archive. Amazon's spam policy describes scaled content abuse, and its application review asks for robust original content (S09, S19). Volume is the failure mode here, not the goal.

From question to page

  1. Pick the question. From measured queries once Search Console is verified (discovery task D-09 FLG-013), and from the queue above before then.
  2. Read the primary sources in full. The manufacturer's page and the listing, both in full. A guide that cites a search excerpt is not finished.
  3. Write down the conflict. If two official pages disagree, the guide publishes the disagreement rather than picking a winner. That rule produced most of what is distinctive on this site.
  4. Pass the gates. The build re-runs tools/verify.mjs: disclosure above the fold, sponsored attributes, tagged links, no prices, every source fetched and dated, sitemap and feed coverage, no broken internal links. A failing check blocks the change (see Compliance).
  5. Publish and index. The guide is added to the sitemap and the RSS feed automatically at build time.

Parallel-session coordination

This repository may be worked on by several agent sessions at once. The shared task board is COORDINATION.md at the repository root: tasks are claimed there, scopes do not overlap, and shared outputs (registers, navigation, the front page) are integrated only by a final integration pass that re-verifies everything. Conflicts are resolved without discarding the other session's work, and the resolution is logged.

What is automated, and what is not

  • Automated: site generation, the compliance gate, the source-freshness gate, link checking, sitemap and feed updates, build-drift detection in CI.
  • Requires the owner's credentials: Associates Central link validation (gate G-02 FLG-005), Search Console verification, analytics-endpoint deployment, any ledger import.
  • Requires a judgment: which question to answer next, and whether a conflict is worth publishing. Neither is delegated.
