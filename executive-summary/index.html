Executive summary

What was built, what was verified, what could not be, and what has to happen next. One page, in that order.

What this is

A reader-facing affiliate publication for two countertop cooking categories, with the machinery that produced it published alongside it. The front page reads like a shop. The register, the source list and the flags read like the notebook behind the shop, and both are one click away.

The five products, and what was confirmed about them

All five submitted links were fetched and their redirects followed to the final destination on amazon.com. Every destination was read directly: its title, its breadcrumb and its date.

The four findings that shaped the build

Finding 1 — none of the five tokens can be an ASIN

Amazon defines an ASIN as a 10-character alphanumeric identifier. Three of the submitted tokens are 9 characters, and every observed destination is a 10-character ASIN. That is why the register keeps intent and observation in two separate tables: the destination we measured and the product that was meant are different facts, and merging them would let a guess look like a verification.

Finding 2 — prices and ratings are not publishable as static text

Program Policies 2(b) allows a price on your site only when Amazon serves the link that displays it or the price comes from Creators API or PA API. 5(t) does the same for star ratings and customer reviews. So this site shows neither, and instead links to the listing. The code path for a live lookup exists and stays empty until the API is configured.

Finding 3 — the submitted links are redirects, not Special Links

Program Policies requires Special Links to be accessed directly from your site and restricts Redirecting Links and link shorteners. The five submitted links are Amazon-hosted redirects, so they are recorded in the register but are not published as outbound links. Every outbound link on this site is a direct Special Link carrying the tag, marked rel sponsored nofollow. Whether each short link is enrolled in the owner's own workflow is private account data and is flagged rather than judged.

Finding 4 — PA-API 5 is dead, and the replacement has a threshold

PA-API 5 is deprecated and now returns an HTTP 403 AccessDeniedException. The Creators API is the supported path, and it requires at least 10 qualifying sales in the past 30 days before it will serve anything. The research tooling targets Creators API from the start for that reason.

What was built

  • A publication front page, 6 question-shaped guides, two category hubs, two comparison pages and a comparison hub — every one of them carrying the disclosure strip and a dated source.
  • A product register with a separate product-intent record and a separate observed redirect record, plus a Flagged for Review page listing 12 items this desk could not settle.
  • A decision board with 18 open questions and 2 closed ones, each with what is known, what is missing and the step that closes it, and a suggestions page with 17 ranked changes and 4 possible shapes for the site.
  • Privacy-preserving measurement: five defined events, a self-hosted collector that stores no IP, no cookie and no advertising id, and a campaign ledger that ships empty with its columns already defined.
  • A build gate that fails the release on a missing disclosure, an unmarked product link, a broken internal link, a missing canonical, or a page missing from the sitemap or the feed.

What is not done

  • The tracking ID is unconfirmed. It matches Amazon's published shape; nothing public confirms it was issued to this account.
  • Creators API access is not configured, so no live price, no rating and no automated product research is running yet.
  • There is no measured traffic data, so no topic is described as popular and no revenue figure appears anywhere.
  • The reader capture form is built but has no list provider behind it.
