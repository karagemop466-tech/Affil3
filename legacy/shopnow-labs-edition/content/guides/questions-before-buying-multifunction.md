## The trap in the preset count

The Emeril Lagasse 26QT listing advertises 24 one-touch presets. The Chefman TurboFry 8-Quart advertises a 4-in-1 with bake, dehydrate and frozen modes.

Neither number tells you whether the appliance is good at those things. A preset is a saved time and temperature. It is a convenience, not a capability. Twenty-four presets on a mediocre convection oven is still a mediocre convection oven, and one well-built basket with a temperature dial can beat it for the food you actually cook.

So the question is not "how many functions". It is "which of these functions will I use weekly, and does the hardware support them".

## Fourteen questions

Grouped by what they are really testing.

### What you will actually use

**1. Which three things will I cook in this most often?**
Write them down before you look at any listing. If a function is not in your top three, it should not influence the purchase.

**2. For each of those, does the appliance do it by design or by preset?**
Dehydrating needs sustained low temperature and airflow. Baking needs even heat and a flat surface. Air frying needs high heat and air movement. Check that the hardware matches the function, not just that a button exists.

**3. Is the capacity right for the function, not just for the appliance?**
A 26-quart oven is good at baking a pan of something. It is less good at crisping a single portion of fries, because a large cavity is a lot of air to heat for a small load. A 3-quart basket is the reverse.

### Whether it will fit your life

**4. Where will it live, permanently?**
Multi-function appliances are heavy. If it lives in a cupboard it will be used a fraction as often as one that lives on the counter.

**5. Does it fit under the cabinet with the door open or the drawer out?**
This is the most common purchase regret in this category. Measure the full working height, not the closed height.

**6. How many parts does a typical cook dirty?**
Count from the "what's in the box" list. Six accessories is six things that can need washing.

**7. Do those parts fit my sink or dishwasher?**
Dimensions, not descriptions. A rack wider than your dishwasher rack is hand-wash work forever.

### Whether the numbers mean anything

**8. What is the stated maximum temperature, and does my cooking need it?**
The Chefman TurboFry lists 450°F. The CHEF iQ Sense probe lists 1000°F heat resistance. Higher is not automatically better; it matters only if you cook things that need it.

**9. Is the temperature accurate, or just adjustable?**
An adjustable dial and an accurate oven are different claims. Nothing in a listing proves accuracy. Reviews that mention a separate oven thermometer are the closest thing to evidence you will get before buying.

**10. What is the stated wattage, and can my kitchen supply it?**
High-wattage appliances on a shared circuit trip breakers when run with a kettle or a microwave. The retrieved listings did not state wattage in the text we captured; check the product details section before ordering.

**11. Does the unit need to preheat, and for how long?**
Preheat time is real cooking time. A review excerpt on the Emeril listing notes it is slow to preheat compared with a previous air fryer. That is one customer's comparison, but it is the kind of detail that changes daily use.

### Whether it will last

**12. What warranty is stated, and who honors it?**
If a listing does not state one, plan as though there is none.

**13. What do the recent one-star reviews say, specifically?**
Not the average. Read the last twenty lowest-rated reviews and look for a repeated mechanical failure rather than a repeated complaint about a preference.

**14. Is a specialist tool better than a multi-function one?**
Often yes. If the problem is doneness, [a probe](/buying-guides/knowing-when-food-is-done/) is cheaper and more accurate than a new appliance. If the problem is batch size for one dish, one big basket beats a multi-function oven.

:::note A useful reframe
"Multi-function" is a claim about the product. "Multi-purpose" is a claim about you. A appliance only earns the second word if its functions match your week, and that is decided by your list, not by the box.
:::

## Decision path

1. **Write your three most frequent cooks.**
2. **Eliminate anything that cannot do those three by hardware.** Presets do not count.
3. **Measure your counter for working clearance**, not closed dimensions.
4. **Count parts and check your sink.**
5. **Read recent one-star reviews** for repeated mechanical failures.
6. **Ask whether a cheaper specialist tool solves the actual problem.**
7. Only then compare models on the same shortlist.

That order matters. Most buyers start at step 7 and work backwards, which is how a 24-preset oven ends up in a cupboard.
