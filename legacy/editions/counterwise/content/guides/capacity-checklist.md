## Why the headline number is the least useful one on the page

Every air fryer listing leads with quarts, because quarts are the easiest number to make big. Capacity is what actually happens to your dinner inside that volume, and four checks separate the two. None of them requires a spec sheet.

The listings we track span six to twenty-six quarts. Here is what the difference is and is not.

| Registered row | Titled capacity | What the title says the capacity is made of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Twin basket | 6 Quart | "2 Independent 3Qt" baskets — a sum of two halves |
| Single basket | 8 Qt | one "XL" basket |
| Oven-style | 26QT | "Extra Large Interior" with accessories and presets |

All three strings are the seller's, captured 2026-11-20, and all three are describing different things. Comparing 6, 8 and 26 as a single column of numbers is the mistake this page exists to prevent.

## Check one: the largest single thing you cook

Write down one item you cook more than twice a month. A tray of wings. Four chicken thighs. A small roast. A frozen bag of something.

Now ask whether that item fits in **one** cooking layer, in **one** basket, without stacking. That question eliminates more machines than price does.

A twin-basket unit's total volume only exists if your food splits evenly across both halves. If your real unit of cooking is "a whole tray of wings", two three-quart baskets are two smaller trays, and you are buying a machine for a household problem you do not have.

## Check two: divide, then re-read the marketing

When capacity is a sum, divide it and read the halves. "6 Quart" becomes "3Qt each", and 3 quarts is a small basket — enough for a couple's dinner, not for company. The gap between the number on the box and the number you'll cook at is where most buyer disappointment lives, and it costs nothing to check first.

The reverse trap exists too. An oven-style cavity may advertise twenty-six quarts while offering a rack area closer to a small toaster oven's, because cavity volume includes all the space above the racks that your food cannot occupy in a single layer. Vertical volume is genuinely useful for a whole bird or a tall cut. For fries, it is mostly air.

## Check three: single layer, in your own words

Crispness comes from moving air reaching the surface of the food. Pile food on itself and the middle steams. So the honest comparison figure is not "how much fits in the basket" but "how much fits in one layer at the bottom of the basket".

Estimate it in your kitchen, not theirs:

- Lay a sheet of paper the size of your baking tray. If that is what fits in the basket, you have the answer.
- Count pieces, not grams. "20 wings in a single layer" beats any quart figure.
- Accept that a batch machine is normal. A machine that cooks two batches of a four-person main is a machine you use twice, not a machine you dislike.

## Check four: for ovens, count positions rather than quarts

If you are looking at oven-style units, ignore the cavity number and ask three questions: how many rack heights fit a tray; can the door stay open without hitting the counter behind it; and does the accessory list actually include the tray and rack you need. Our oven-style row is titled as including "Six Cooking Accessories" — worth a look, because accessories are the difference between a machine that arrived complete and one that needs an order on top of it.

## A rule of thumb with no marketing in it

For two people, one 3-to-5-quart basket is usually enough, and the extra capacity will be spent on preheating air. For four, a 6-to-8-quart single basket or one oven-style cavity is the honest minimum for one-pass cooking. For six or more, buy a bigger cavity or accept batches; two baskets help only when your menu has two components that finish together.

That is a judgement about households, not about products. No listing on our register is being recommended by it.

## What this page does not tell you

We do not know how much food these machines hold, because we have not cooked in them and we did not find a verified capacity figure in the pages we captured — only the sellers' title strings. Depth, width, rack spacing and tray sizes are blank on our gear pages for the same reason. Prices change hourly and are never printed here; the listing shows the live one.
