How we pick

The method, before the recommendations

We match an appliance's design to the job you actually have. Where the listing does not give us what we need to make the match, we say so.

Updated 2026-11-20Method applies to all six guides

The order of operations

  1. Start from the job. What do you cook most often, for how many people, in what size of pan or tray?
  2. Choose the shape. Dual basket, single basket, oven, or an instrument instead of an appliance. This decision eliminates more products than any specification comparison.
  3. Check the space. Footprint, clearance above, clearance in front. A product that does not fit its home is not a candidate.
  4. Count the cleanup. Parts that touch grease, and whether they fit your sink.
  5. Read the specifications as stated. Not as summarised, not as inferred.
  6. Read the objections. Recent one-star reviews, stated warranty, and any repeated mechanical complaint.
  7. Ask whether a cheaper specialist tool solves it. Sometimes the answer is a probe, not an appliance.

Why we do not produce a ranked "best" list

A ranking implies that one product is best across all buyers, which is only true when everybody cooks the same thing in the same kitchen. Two 3-quart drawers beat an 8-quart basket for some households and lose badly for others. Instead of a ranking, each guide maps designs to jobs and states which job each product fits.

What counts as evidence here

  • Listing specifications — the strongest thing we have, because they are the manufacturer's own statements.
  • Amazon's review summaries — reported as what the widget displayed, never as our finding.
  • Compliance statements — quoted from Amazon, the FTC, or 16 CFR, with the section and the retrieval date.
  • Industry commentary — not used as evidence for any claim. Where a claim exists only in secondary reporting, it appears as an open question instead of a published fact.

What would change our recommendations

Measured performance data would change them immediately, which is why question 8 asks whether there is any budget for testing. Real search query data would change which guides come next, which is why question 5 is about Search Console.

What we won't do

Publish a price we cannot source. Rank by commission. Describe a product we have not read the listing for. Fill a blank with an estimate to make a table look complete. Every blank on this site is blank on purpose, and is listed on the known gaps page.
